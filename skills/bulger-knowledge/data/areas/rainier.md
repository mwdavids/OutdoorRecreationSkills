# Mount Rainier – Emmons Glacier Ski Descent (Washington Cascades)

## What it is
The Emmons Glacier on Mount Rainier's northeast flank is the largest glacier by surface area in the contiguous United States, offering one of the biggest vertical ski descents in Washington with almost 5,000 feet from the summit to Camp Schurman and an additional 3,000 feet down the Interglacier. This is a demanding single-day or multi-day ski mountaineering objective requiring glacier travel skills, crevasse rescue competency, and the fitness to manage 10,000 vertical feet round-trip while carrying skis on exposed terrain above 11,000 feet.

## Standard line
- White River Campground trailhead (4,400 ft), accessed via White River Road 5 miles from SR-410
- 3.5 miles to Glacier Basin (5,935 ft), another mile to Inter Glacier base (7,000 ft), then 1,500 feet up Inter Glacier to Camp Curtis (9,000 ft), traversing descent to Emmons Glacier and ascent to Camp Schurman (9,500 ft)
- From Schurman, follow "The Corridor"—a smooth 30-degree ramp ascending to approximately 11,200 feet, then traverse right on 35-40 degree slopes to skirt the summit cap and ascend through crevasses to the crater rim
- Skiers typically skin through most of The Corridor to 11,000–11,500 feet before booting; descent is entirely on skis for competent parties
- Standard approach: 2-day with camp at Schurman; car-to-car in 13–17 hours for fit parties

## Season
- Recommended climbing timeframe is mid-May to September, but ski conditions favor early season (May through June) with better snow coverage, cooler/wetter weather, and fewer crevasses
- White River Road access remains seasonally closed until late May, with the National Park Service estimating opening around Memorial Day weekend
- May and June offer more direct routes to the summit with intact snow bridges; by mid-summer, glacial acceleration and melting snowpack create routefinding challenges
- The Emmons typically becomes trickier to navigate by early August due to hardened blue ice, opened crevasses, and trickier approaches
- For a May 16 objective: viable but early—road may be barely open, approach will have continuous snow, and freezing levels remain low for good overnight refreeze

## Hazards
- NWAC avalanche forecasts cover terrain below 8,000 feet only, from late November to mid-April—no avalanche forecast exists for the upper mountain; climbers must self-assess
- NWAC zone: **cascade-west-central** (Mount Rainier falls under West Slopes Central forecast area)
- Crevasse falls are most common on Emmons Glacier; the route has been relatively free of avalanche or falling ice compared to other Rainier routes
- Little Tahoma's North Face randomly discharges rocks down the lower Emmons; rockfall risk when ascending/descending late in the day or in rainy conditions
- Upper Emmons route changes yearly based on crevasses; earlier in the year it tends to go straight up, later takes a long righthand traverse to end-run a massive crevasse
- Weather systems close in quickly from the west side, giving east-side climbers no warning
- Bergschrund at upper mountain can be open or bridged depending on season
- Single-day parties face afternoon sun-softened snow and heightened crevasse visibility issues on descent

## Disambiguation
- **This IS**: Emmons Glacier route on Mount Rainier's east side, starting from White River Campground (46.8523°N, 121.7603°W approximate trailhead), ascending via Inter Glacier → Camp Schurman → The Corridor → summit crater
- **This is NOT**: The Disappointment Cleaver (DC) route on Rainier's south side from Paradise, nor the Kautz or Liberty Ridge routes
- Second most climbed route on Rainier, receiving about 15% of the approximately 10,000 annual summit attempts
- Canonical sources: Green Trails Mt Rainier East No. 270; The Mountaineers / RMI / SummitPost route pages

## What this is NOT
- Not a route for first-time glacier travelers—requires full crevasse rescue systems and competency
- Not consistently wanded or bootpacked like the DC route; DC receives much more traffic and is maintained by guide services
- One of the easiest routes on Rainier technically, but long and strenuous with 10,000 vertical feet demanding solid aerobic conditioning
- Not the Winthrop Glacier proper (though often called "Emmons-Winthrop" as the route skirts both glaciers near the summit)
- Single-day car-to-car ski descents are aggressive; most parties camp at Schurman

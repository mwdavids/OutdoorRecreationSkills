# Mount Shuksan via Sulphide Glacier (North Cascades)

## What it is
Mount Shuksan is a glaciated massif at 9,131 feet in North Cascades National Park, accessed via a 7-mile forest approach to a gentle glacier climb that ends with a 500-foot snow (early season) or rock scramble up the summit pyramid. The Sulphide Glacier Route demands approximately 6,700 feet of elevation gain from trailhead to summit, combining moderate glacier travel with a 600-foot, Class 3 rock scramble up the pyramid's central gully. This is the most popular and moderate route on Shuksan, typically climbed as a 2-3 day expedition with high camp established at 5,400–6,500 feet near the glacier's edge.

## Standard line
- Shannon Ridge Trailhead via SR-20 to Baker Lake Road, 23 miles to Shannon Creek Campground, then FR 1152 and FR 1152-014 to road end at 2,500 feet
- Hike old road 2 miles to ~3,800 feet, then switchback to forested ridge, north 0.75 miles to timberline at ~4,600 feet, then 2 miles along flat crest to pass at ~5,400 feet; traverse northeast to Sulphide Glacier terminus; ascend glacier northwest 3–4 hours to base of summit pyramid at ~8,400 feet
- Continue up Class 3 rock gully (snow in early season) to summit at 9,127–9,131 feet; descend via rappel and downclimb back to glacier
- 14 miles roundtrip; typically 5–6 hours trailhead to camp, 5–7 hours camp to summit and return

## Season
- July to August is the most popular time of year to visit, with April through June offering best conditions for ski mountaineering
- Mid-June is the sweet spot for traditional climbing: summit pyramid transitions from snow (early season) to dry rock scrambling, crevasses are still bridged but visible, and alpine meadows are scattered with wildflowers
- Earlier (April–May) requires skiing or steep snow climbing; access roads may be snow-covered below trailhead
- Later (August–September) sees late-afternoon surface softening become hazardous on glacier; conditions deteriorate in afternoon with lots of ice and open crevasses; summit rock becomes dry but loose

## Hazards
- **NWAC Zone**: West Slopes North (Mount Baker area); persistent slab, wind slab, and wet loose problems dominate spring/early summer; moderate avalanche risk typical on approach slopes below 5,400 feet
- **Crevasses**: Glacier has many crevasses late-season; no established bootpack on lower Sulphide when conditions are icy, requiring careful navigation; whole lower Sulphide can become a no-fall zone in morning ice
- **Rockfall**: Take special care on summit pyramid gully because of loose rock and multiple parties
- **Summit conditions**: If rock is covered with ice, do not attempt summit; transition from snow to rock at summit crux is exposed 
- **Permits**: Only 6 parties allowed per day at high camps; permits first-come, first-served beginning day before
- **Weather**: Afternoon storms common; pre-dawn alpine starts (2–4am) standard to avoid soft snow and afternoon hazards

## Disambiguation
- **This IS**: Mount Shuksan (48.8310°N, -121.6029°W) via Sulphide Glacier (south side), accessed from Shannon Ridge Trailhead off Baker Lake Road; Shuksan rises immediately east of Mount Baker, 11.6 miles south of Canada–US border
- **NOT**: Fisher Chimneys Route (north side, more technical); Price Glacier Route (west side, first-ascent route); Curtis Glacier routes (southwest); White Salmon Glacier approaches
- **Canonical sources**: Beckey's *Cascade Alpine Guide* Vol. 3; Green Trails Mt Shuksan No. 14; Green Trails Mount Baker Wilderness Climbing No. 13SX; Mountaineers route database

## What this is NOT
- This is NOT a non-technical scramble: glacier travel, crevasse rescue, and Class 3–4 rock skills are mandatory
- This is NOT skiable to the summit in summer: ski descents are spring objectives (April–June), descending 4,000+ vertical feet
- Do not confuse the "gentle" glacier description with non-glaciated: it is possible to ascend from snow-covered moraine onto crevassed glacier without realizing it
- Not to be mistaken for an Artist Point viewpoint hike (Mount Shuksan is visible from there but 10+ miles away)

# Mount Stuart via Cascadian Couloir (Central Cascades, WA)

## What it is
Mount Stuart is the second highest non-volcanic peak in Washington, at 9,415 ft in the Alpine Lakes Wilderness. The Cascadian Couloir is the standard route from the south and the most climbed route on the peak—a non-technical Class 3 scramble up over 4,600 vertical feet of scree, talus, loose rock, and seasonal snow. The standard route is the Class 3 Southeast Route up the Cascadian Couloir, requiring alpine start discipline, strong routefinding, and tolerance for monotonous scree slog punctuated by rockfall hazard.

## Standard line
- Esmeralda Trailhead at 4,243 ft (end of Forest Road 9737)
- Hike old jeep road 0.5 mi, fork right onto Ingalls Way, switchback up Fortune Peak to Longs Pass (6,300 ft)
- Descend northeast into basin at 4,800 ft, hike 0.25 mi southeast on Ingalls Creek Trail to meadow at base of couloir (4,600 vertical above)
- Ascend Cascadian Couloir, 700 ft of elevation gain to proper couloir entrance, then steep scree/talus/snow gully with serious rockfall hazard from climbers above
- Top out at false summit (9,200 ft), scramble Class 2-3 broken rock along left side of ridge to true summit (9,415 ft)
- 11.1 miles roundtrip, 6,309 ft gain from Esmeralda via Longs Pass
- Day-trip: 3-4 hr trailhead to camp, 5-8 hr camp to summit; strong parties routinely do car-to-car in 12–14 hours

## Season
- **Sweet spot: Late May through early July for snow climbing in late spring/early summer**
- Best as a snow climb in late spring and early summer. Steep snow early season (bring crampons); rockfall dangers after snowmelt. Ice axe and crampons recommended most of the year; snow usually melts out through July.
- **Too early**: Road may be closed into June. Early season, section between false and true summit heavily corniced.
- **Too late**: Long day up hot, south-facing scree couloir; early season more enjoyable on snow than gravel. Bring at least 3 liters of water if climbing in late summer. Rockfall danger increases significantly after snow melts.

## Hazards
- **Avalanche character**: Stuart Range sits approximately 15-20 miles east of the Cascade Crest, in the East Central zone (NWAC cascade-east-central). Spring wind slab and wet loose avalanches in the couloir during snowmelt. Section between false and true summit normally heavily corniced early season.
- **Rockfall**: Biggest danger is the human factor—watch for rocks falling from climbers above; try not to knock rocks down. Notorious for extremely loose, unstable rock, sand, scree creating serious rockfall hazard; helmet highly recommended. Until approximately 10 AM first 3,000 ft is in full shade, reducing but not eliminating rockfall risk.
- **Other objective hazards**: Day-trip requires alpine start to avoid heat of the day and maximize daylight. Water scarce above Ingalls Creek. Band of otherwise impenetrable slide alder en-route to couloir base.
- **Non-hazards**: Leave rope, helmet, harness and pro at home—this is a scramble route (though helmet strongly advised for rockfall). No glacier travel required. No permit required for southern routes; no Enchantments permit needed for camping south of summit.

## Disambiguation
- **This objective IS**: Mount Stuart (9,415 ft), highest peak in Stuart Range, Alpine Lakes Wilderness, central Washington Cascades south of Stevens Pass and east of Snoqualmie Pass. Coordinates: 47.4751°N, -120.9031°W.
- **NOT**: Lake Ingalls day hike; Sherpa Glacier approaches from north side; technical routes (West Ridge 5.6, North Ridge 5.7, Ice Cliff Glacier); Ulrich's Couloir (steeper variation west of Cascadian).
- **Canonical sources**: Fred Beckey, Cascade Alpine Guide Vol. 3: "Without a rival as the crown peak in the central Cascades". Green Trails Mt Stuart No. 209.

## What this is NOT
- **NOT a beginner scramble**: The easiest way up Mount Stuart, but that doesn't make it easy—over 4,600 vertical feet of Class 3 rock scrambling, and more difficult yet, the same 4,600 ft of descending. Descriptions don't capture how sketchy and exposed some terrain is toward summit; be mindful of footing.
- **NOT well-marked**: 10 trails going in 15 directions randomly splattered with cairns. Trails sprout off Ingalls Creek Trail about every 100 yards; entrance to Cascadian Couloir not clearly visible from meadow.
- **NOT a rope-up objective**: Parties carrying rope and climbing gear typically don't end up using it, but steep snowfield near false summit (approaching 45°) in early season may warrant rope for less-experienced parties.

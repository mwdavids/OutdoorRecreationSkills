## Summary
Mount Fernow is one of the highest Bulgers and sits between Seven Fingered Jack and Copper at the converging heads of the Entiat River (E), Copper Creek (NE), and Big Creek (W). It is distinctly alpine but, being east of the main crest, has limited modern glaciation. Small glacier remnants remain in the Maude-7FJ-Fernow cirque, in the Copper Creek drainage, and on the north side, all much reduced versus historical extents.

Standard non-technical lines are mostly Class 3 with loose terrain, routefinding, and significant cumulative effort. The mountain is often approached from Phelps Creek (most practical drive + shortest overall approach), Holden Village (ferry logistics), or Entiat Meadows (very long valley approach).

## Best-Season Window
- Primary window: late July to September for the standard Class 3 routes when snow coverage on ledges/gullies is reduced.
- Shoulder season: June to early July can work with snow travel skills; expect snow in traverses/couloirs and possible crampon/axe use.
- Winter/early spring: generally a specialty objective with serious avalanche/snowpack implications and much longer effort.

Candidate seasons: summer, fall

## Standard Route
Recommended standard for most parties: Phelps Creek -> Leroy Basin -> 7FJ west-ridge saddle -> Big Creek head traverse -> SW couloir -> south col -> summit (Class 3, loose/alpine snow depending on season).

Typical effort profile: full-value alpine day for very strong teams, or a 2-day trip with a high camp in Leroy Basin.

### Approach
Phelps Creek TH (3500 ft) via Chiwawa River Road system near Trinity:
- From US-2 at Coles Corner, head toward Lake Wenatchee, then onto Chiwawa River Road (FS 62), continue to Phelps Creek Road and TH.
- Hike Phelps Creek Trail about 3.5 mi to Leroy Creek crossing (~4120 ft).
- Take climber's path up N side of Leroy Creek into Leroy Basin.
- Good high camp: meadow around 6100 ft SW of Seven Fingered Jack (water usually available in season).
- Lower camp option: Phelps Creek trail near Leroy crossing for lighter summit push logistics, but less scenic.

### Summit Route
From ~6100 ft Leroy Basin:
- Climb NNE up drainage on west side of Seven Fingered Jack to saddle on W Ridge of 7FJ (~7760 ft).
- Descend roughly 800-1000 ft into head of Big Creek (the big "lose it to regain it" section).
- Traverse under glacier remnant and aim for broad SW valley/couloir on Fernow.
- Ascend shallow SW couloir (often snow-filled) to ~8700 ft col immediately south of summit.
- Finish on easiest Class 3 terrain, commonly circling around E side counterclockwise on ribs/gullies.

Cruxes:
- Getting off 7FJ saddle into Big Creek basin: often reported Class 3; some parties call it low Class 4 depending on exact line/conditions.
- Routefinding in cliffs/ribs around the SW approach and summit finish, especially in poor visibility.

### Descent
Normally reverse the ascent route:
- Downclimb summit Class 3 terrain to south col.
- Descend SW couloir and traverse back into Big Creek head.
- Regain 7FJ west-ridge saddle and descend to Leroy Basin/Phelps.

Key descent tax:
- Late-day regain back to the 7FJ saddle is strenuous and time-consuming.
- Snow softening can increase slide hazard in couloir/traverse zones.

## Equipment
- Summer/fall dry conditions: helmet, trekking poles, sturdy footwear, navigation tools, emergency layer, and water filtering setup.
- Shoulder season/snow: ice axe and crampons strongly recommended; microspikes are often insufficient for firm alpine snow.
- Rope/protection: usually not required for standard Class 3 line in favorable summer conditions, but light rope can be prudent for conservative teams when saddle descent is icy/exposed.
- North Face/ice variation: technical ice kit required (not a standard route).

## Alternates / Variations
1. Holden Village -> Copper Creek -> Copper-Entiat Divide (7200 ft) -> Fernow East Ridge south-side talus line (Class 3).
2. Entiat Meadows -> climber path to Fernow-Buckskin saddle (7200 ft) -> East Ridge south-side finish (Class 3).
3. North Face/West Ridge finish from Big Creek side (technical snow/ice mountaineering; steeper and more committing).

## Access Notes & Permits
Phelps Creek approach:
- Most practical approach for drive + overall distance.

- Requires Lake Chelan ferry logistics (Lady of the Lake) to Holden access network.

Entiat approach:

## Tactical Notes
- Early alpine start is critical due to cumulative terrain and major elevation loss/regain.
- Largest strategic cost on Phelps line is descent into Big Creek head then reclimb to 7FJ saddle on exit.
- Whiteout risk: routefinding is straightforward in clear weather but can become confusing in cliff/talus terrain.
- Objective hazards: loose rock, snow fingers over hard surfaces, late-day soft snow, and minor crevasse features near glacier remnants.
- Water/camps: reliable options around Leroy Basin meadow (~6100 ft) in normal season.
- Team composition: small, efficient party helps in loose terrain and routefinding transitions.

## Combinations
- Fernow + Seven Fingered Jack linkups are possible for very strong teams in stable conditions.
- Fernow can be paired with broader Maude/7FJ/Fernow cirque traversing objectives depending on snow and camp placement.
- Entiat-side itineraries may combine with Buckskin-area objectives, but mileage is significant.

# Gardner Mountain

**Bulger #22 · 8,901.8 ft (LIDAR) · Prom 716 ft · Lake Chelan–Sawtooth Wilderness Highpoint**

## Status
Unclimbed (as of April 2026; user queried April 20, 2026).

## Personal Context
Gardner Mountain is typically climbed **in combo with North Gardner Mountain** (0.83 mi away, separated by ~800 ft). Most parties do both peaks in a single summit day from Gardner Meadows high camp. It is the **Lake Chelan–Sawtooth Wilderness Highpoint** — a prominence bump on the crest between North Gardner and a western col. Standard route is an easy Class 1–2 scramble on open, south-facing slopes that dry out quickly in May–June, making it an excellent early-season objective.

**Elevation:** LIDAR 8,901.8 ft (PeakBagger, revised from JSON 8,897 ft). **Prominence:** Clean prom 716.0 ft (line parent North Gardner; revised from JSON 698 ft). **Key col:** "Gardner West Col" at 8,185.8 ft (saddle between Gardner and North Gardner).

## Best-Season Window
**Late May through mid-October** (shared with North Gardner; see [north-gardner.md](north-gardner.md) for detailed seasonal context).

- **Optimal (late May – early June):** South-facing slopes dried of winter snow but upper slopes still retain refrozen crust. Continuous snow coverage on Wolf Creek Trail minimizes bushwhacking to Gardner Meadows. Excellent for early-season parties. Two-day trip standard.
- **Summer (July–October):** Fully snow-free; scree firm. Three-day trip allows leisure pacing. September brings cooler temperatures and lower bugs, and late September into early October can be especially beautiful when larches in the Gardner country start to turn.
- **Not recommended before late May or after mid-November:** Avalanche hazard or inconsistent snow coverage.

## Standard Route: South Slope via Wolf Creek
**Distance:** ~12–14 miles RT from trailhead (shared ~10 mi approach to Gardner Meadows, then ~1–2 mi off-trail to summit and back). **Elevation gain:** ~7,000–9,000 ft total (shared ~3,000 ft approach + ~4,000 ft ascent from meadows). **Class:** 1–2 scramble. **Time:** 3 days (overnight at Gardner Meadows + high camp or return camp).

### Approach: Wolf Creek Trail to Gardner Meadows
Shared with North Gardner. See [north-gardner.md](north-gardner.md) Approach section.
- **Trailhead:** Wolf Creek TH (2,950 ft), well-graded gravel road west of Winthrop.
- **Trail:** ~10 miles to Gardner Meadows, ~2,950 ft gain.
- **First camp:** Gardner Meadows (~6,000 ft), 6–7 hours from TH.

### Summit Day: Gardner Meadows → Gardner (South Slope)
**From camp:** Ascend grassy slopes **due east/northeast** from Gardner Meadows, staying west of a small stream draining from Gardner. Aim for ridgeline at ~6,200 ft (small meadow basin); work uphill, staying west of stream. Contend with a few gullies and negotiate around minor cliff bands, eventually gaining the ridgeline. 

**Upper slopes:** Broad, south-facing, dry of snow by late June (except exceptional years). Mostly scree and rock, trending Class 1–2. No significant scrambling crux. Open, rolling terrain.

**Summit:** Reached in ~2.5–3 hours from camp (May timing with careful footing on steep scree; late summer slightly faster on stable dry scree). Small summit; views comparable to North Gardner (panoramic North Cascades, Chelan-Sawtooths, surrounding ridges).

**Summit RT from camp:** ~5–6 hours (including traverse to North Gardner and return).

## Combo with North Gardner (Standard Ascent Order)
**Recommended order (per Lucas Mountain Knowledge, May 2025 & TrailCatJim, June 2018):**
1. **Gardner first (south slopes):** Warm sun, scree-dry by late morning. Rapid ascent. From Gardner Meadows, ascend SE/S to summit via south slope route (described above), ~2.5 hrs.
2. **Traverse to North Gardner:** From Gardner summit, drop to **Gardner West Col** (the saddle ~8,186 ft between peaks). Terrain is Class 2–3 ridge scramble; one **mandatory ice-axe worthy slope** if spring snow present (late May–early June); late-season parties boot-scramble. ~0.5–1 hr traverse depending on conditions and snow-crossing skill. Ridge offers fun scrambling; stay on or left (west) of crest to avoid most exposure.
3. **North Gardner ascent:** Easy Class 1–2 to true summit from ridge. ~0.5 hrs.
4. **Descent:** From North Gardner summit, retrace ridge traverse back to Gardner summit (~1 hr), then descend Gardner south slope. **Scree-surf capability:** Late July–August parties report excellent scree-surf descent from Point 8,487 false summit region on North Gardner side; not available via Gardner direct descent.

**Combined combo:** 5–6 hours RT from Gardner Meadows; moderate pace with good photography/view stops.

**Alternative (avoid Gardner traverse crux):**
- Ascend Gardner south slope, summit Gardner, return to meadows. Skip North Gardner. 3–4 hours RT.
- Or ascend to North Gardner via Wolf Creek Basin route, skip Gardner entirely (though Gardner is easy bonus).

## Equipment
Same as North Gardner:
- **Ice axe & crampons** (May–mid-June for ridge traverse snow crossing)
- **Microspikes or trail runners** (late summer)
- **Trekking poles** (long descent)
- **Standard 3-season pack** (30–40L for 2–3 day trip)

## Tactical Notes
- **South-facing slopes + early morning = best:** Gardner's south-facing slopes are first to dry in spring and retain fewest snow patches late summer. Start early (5–6 AM) from Gardner Meadows to maximize daylight on lower-angled traverse section and ensure stable afternoon scree.
- **Counterclockwise order preferred:** Ascend Gardner, traverse north to North Gardner, descend via North Gardner's upper bowl / Point 8,487 scree-surf zone. This maximizes scree-run and keeps warmer south face as exit route.
- **Solo or party of 2 best:** Confined ridge scramble between peaks; larger parties should move efficiently to avoid rope-tangle.

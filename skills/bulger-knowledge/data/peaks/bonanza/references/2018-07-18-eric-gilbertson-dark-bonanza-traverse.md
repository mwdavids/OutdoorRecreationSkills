# Reference TR — Eric Gilbertson, Dark-Bonanza-Martin traverse, 2018-07-17/18

← Peak file: [bonanza.md](../../bonanza.md)

## Source
Eric Gilbertson (Country Highpoints) — `https://www.countryhighpoints.com/dark-peak-bonanza-peak-and-martin-peak/`

Eric and Duncan, July 17–18, 2018. Dark Peak (8,504') + Bonanza Peak (9,511') + Martin Peak (8,511') in two days from Stehekin to Holden.

## Trip summary
- **Day 1 (Jul 17):** Drive to Chelan → fast ferry to Stehekin → NPS bus to High Bridge → south on PCT 12.5 mi to Hemlock Camp → leave PCT, ascend NW ridge of Dark Peak through open old-growth forest (3,500 ft gain, basically no undergrowth — "more like open forest walking") → bivy on rocks just below the Dark Glacier (~10pm)
- **Day 2 (Jul 18):** Up at sunrise → Dark Glacier (still consolidated, unroped) → Dark Peak summit by 8am → traverse to Bonanza (6 hrs) → descend NE ridge + east face → Mary Green Glacier → Holden Pass (~6:30pm) → Eric soloed Martin Peak in the remaining daylight → camp at Holden Pass
- **Day 3:** Continued to Copper Peak (separate TR)

## Beta relevant to Bonanza

### Approach to Dark Peak (the *good* alternate to Swamp Creek)
- Per Jake Robinson beta: leave PCT at **Hemlock Camp** (~12.5 mi south of High Bridge on PCT)
- Cut left into the woods, ascend NW ridge of Dark Peak
- "Amazingly the forest was basically completely open, with almost no undergrowth. I would hardly even call it bushwhacking, more like open forest walking."
- 3,500 ft of climb, then class 2 to the ridge, drop down to snow on other side for bivy
- **Strong correction:** Most guidebooks/online resources still list **Swamp Creek** as the standard Dark Peak approach. Eric tried it in 2017 (with Katie) — "battled our way through the jungle of slide alder and vine maple", got turned back. Don't use Swamp Creek.

### Dark Glacier
- Mid-July, "still early enough in the season that crevasse danger was minimal" — they continued unroped on styrofoam snow
- Class 2/3 from top of glacier to Dark Peak summit

### Dark-Bonanza ridge traverse (~6 hours)
- Long knife-edge ridge, mostly **4th and low 5th class** with some exposed sections — Blake Herrington rates 5.8+ but Eric & Duncan didn't climb anything that hard, possibly took a variation
- Right (west) side of ridge: more lichen-covered, looser
- Left (east) side: more solid, less lichen, but significantly more exposure down to the Company Glacier
- They generally stayed right (less exposed), moved left when right got committing
- Used a 40m rope, tied in to both ends, simulclimbed with occasional gear around horns
- Bring water — they ran out, refilled from a snow patch on the west side near point 8599
- **Crux:** the ridge from Bonanza's west peak to the true summit. Very sharp, very exposed both sides. They roped up, simulclimbed two pitches with horn-pro, then **belayed one pitch (~5.6) just below the summit**. Used a micro traxion above the crux.

### Bonanza descent — NE ridge → east face → Mary Green Glacier
- NE ridge scrambling, then downclimb the east face on "line of least resistance", rated **class 3+**
- "Sort of exposed, but not so much that you would fall far if you slipped"
- Passed rap anchors but downclimbed to save time
- Reached head of Mary Green Glacier, transitioned to crampons + axes
- **"This glacier had some visible large crevasses and we decided to rope up."**
- Eric used a GPS track from peakbagger (likely Brent Wiencke's)

### Mary Green Glacier exit (the descent crux to flag)
- Bottom of glacier exits onto rock slabs
- "It was late in the day and the snow melt was creating streams covering all the slabs"
- Looked descendable but dicey, especially without knowing the exact route up
- GPS track went farther skiers' left to a group of trees with a rap anchor
- Backed up the anchor, rapped down — **40m rope did NOT reach the bottom**, stopped on a ledge ~15 ft above ground, unroped, downclimbed the rest
- Eric: "**A 60m rope definitely would have reached.**"
- → For any standard Mary Green Glacier route (ascent or descent), bring a 60m rope

### Martin Peak (bonus, solo)
- Very close to Holden Pass, west ridge route
- Mostly class 3, loose; one short 4th-class step just below the summit (good holds, not very exposed)
- Some parties rope up for the 4th-class step (rap anchor present)
- "Complicated" routefinding — gain ridge up gullies, scramble right side, repeat. Cairns help.

## Conditions snapshot
- Mid-July 2018, weather "good"
- Dark Glacier: still consolidated, low crevasse hazard, unroped travel feasible
- Mary Green Glacier: visible large crevasses, rope team mandatory
- Mary Green exit slabs: wet with afternoon snowmelt streams
- Bugs at Holden Pass: "kind of bad"

## How to adapt for a Bonanza-only ascent
- The Mary Green Glacier section + east face descent are exactly the **standard route in reverse** — useful as ascent beta
- For a one-peak Bonanza trip, **flip the logistics**: Lucerne ferry → Holden Village bus → up Railroad Creek → Holden Lake / Holden Pass camp → up Mary Green Glacier → east face → summit → reverse
- Bring 60m rope for the slabs rappel
- Mid-July is a confirmed-good window for Mary Green Glacier conditions (crevasses still bridged but consolidated)

## Caveats
- This was a **traverse from Dark, not a Holden-side ascent** — Eric did not ascend the Mary Green Glacier slabs, only descended them. The exact ascent route through the slabs is not described first-hand here.
- 2018 is a now-old data point on glacier coverage; bridges may have receded since
- Eric & Duncan are strong, fast climbers — their pacing (6 hrs Dark-to-Bonanza traverse, summit Martin solo at end of day) is upper-end
- Blake Herrington's 5.8+ grade for the traverse may reflect a more direct line than the variation Eric & Duncan took

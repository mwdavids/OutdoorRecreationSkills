# Mount Buckner (9,114 ft, Bulger #11)

## Personal status
- **Climbed:** no
- **Attempts:** 1 — most recent [2026-05-04 — turned at Sahale-Boston Col](buckner/2026-05-04-attempt.md)
- **Style preference:** ski mountaineering, N Face

## Summary
Big N Cascades alpine objective in the Cascade Pass / Boston Basin area. Spring ski mountaineering of the N Face is the prize line, but the *approach* — specifically gaining the Boston Glacier — is the real difficulty in May/early June, not the face itself.

## Best-season window
- **Spring N Face ski (mid-April to mid-May):** good face conditions, but gated by whichever col approach is in shape that year
- **Summer N Face / Park Creek Pass (July–August):** col traverses melt out, becomes a more conventional alpine outing
- The JSON's `best_seasons: ["spring"]` is correct in spirit but underspecified — the limiter is the Boston Glacier approach

## Approach options

Three fundamentally different ways onto the Boston Glacier (which all routes ultimately need). Choice is driven by which col is in shape and how much time you have.

- **Sahale-Boston Col (standard spring):** shortest line; Cascade Pass → Sahale Arm → col → onto upper Boston Glacier. Limited by the col traverse being impassable steep snow in many May/early-June years (verified blown on [2026-05-04](buckner/2026-05-04-attempt.md)).
- **Sharkfin Col (Boston Basin alternate):** ~3-4 mi extra one-way; bypasses the Sahale-Boston Col traverse entirely. Snow couloir + short rappel. Realistically 2-day trip with bivy in Boston Basin. Best window mid-April to mid-May before alder/rockfall on the approach.
- **Park Creek Pass (Stehekin summer):** longest, avoids both cols. Only realistic for a summer ascent or when both cols are blown. Logistics from Stehekin still need researching.

**Or skip the Boston Glacier entirely:** the **SW face from Horseshoe Basin** route (see below) reaches the summit without ever touching either col or the Boston Glacier. Trade-off: you give up the N Face ski line, but you get a viable winter and shoulder-season option.

## Standard route — N Face via Sahale-Boston Col
Eldorado TH → Cascade River Rd gate (currently closed at MP 20 per NPS 9/30/2025) → bike/walk to Cascade Pass TH → Sahale Arm → Sahale-Boston Col → traverse onto upper Boston Glacier → Buckner N Face → reverse.

The textbook spring approach. **The col traverse is a real spring crux** — see Crux/hazards below; verified impassable on [2026-05-04](buckner/2026-05-04-attempt.md).

## Alternate route — N Face via Sharkfin Col (Boston Basin approach)
Eldorado TH → Boston Basin Trail → Boston Basin → Sharkfin Col (right-side snow couloir, rappel notch) → Boston Glacier east → Buckner N Face → reverse via Sharkfin Col.

- **When to use:** when the Sahale-Boston Col is loaded with impassable steep snow (typical late-April through mid-June many years)
- **Penalty:** ~3–4 mi extra one-way; realistically a 2-day trip with bivy in Boston Basin (a fast strong party could do c2c in 16–18+ hrs)
- **Best window:** mid-April to mid-May, possibly into early June. Boston Basin Trail melts out and rockfall in the col approach gully starts late May / early June.
- **Permit:** Boston Basin cross-country zone permit (NOCA) required for any bivy on Torment-Forbidden-Boston-Sahale ridgeline or on Boston/Forbidden Glaciers
- Detailed third-party beta on the Sharkfin Col approach: [Climber Kyle Forbidden Tour, 2024-03-30](buckner/references/2024-03-30-climber-kyle-forbidden-tour.md)

## Alternate route — Park Creek Pass (Stehekin side)
Long, Stehekin-side approach that avoids both Sahale-Boston and Sharkfin cols. Best treated as a summer or stable-weather option when col-based entries are out.

- **When to use:** when both col entries are unsafe or out of condition, or for a non-ski summer ascent strategy.
- **Trade-off:** significantly longer logistics and approach complexity than Cascade Pass-side options.
- **Status:** approach logistics from Stehekin (ferry schedule, road access, mileage) still need a focused research pass.

## Alternate route — SW face from Horseshoe Basin (no Boston Glacier)
Eldorado TH → Cascade Pass → Sahale Arm → Sahale Camp → ski/downclimb the SE bowl down into Horseshoe Basin (~6,500 ft) → traverse to SW face of Buckner → climb SW face to SE ridge → summit block.

- **When to use:** any season the SE bowl ski-down is in (Feb through May per Tim Gibson video + Eric Gilbertson Feb 2024); when both cols are out; when N Face conditions don't justify the col gamble; or as a paired Buckner+Horseshoe day from a Sahale Camp basecamp.
- **What you give up:** the N Face ski line. This is a climbing route, not a ski-mountaineering descent of the prize line.
- **SE bowl ski-down (Sahale Camp → Horseshoe Basin):** route through cliff bands hard skiers' right; needs snow coverage; timed for after sunrise to assess. Re-ascent via boots or ascent plates (skin track too steep, booting too deep without plates).
- **SW face climb itself:** moderate steep snow; watch for isolated wind-slab pockets in winter — stick to edges between flattish areas below cliffs.
- **Bonus:** can pair Buckner + Horseshoe in one day from Sahale Camp basecamp (Eric did Horseshoe FWA + Buckner in a 19-hr push on 2024-02-03).
- Detailed beta: [Eric Gilbertson winter ascent, 2024-02-03](buckner/references/2024-02-03-eric-gilbertson-winter-buckner-horseshoe-sahale.md)

## Access & logistics
- **Trailheads:** Eldorado TH (Sahale-Boston Col approach) or Boston Basin TH at MP 22 (Sharkfin Col approach)
- **Cascade River Rd:** Closed at MP 20 (Eldorado TH) as of 9/30/2025 per NPS alert; ~2 mi walk/bike to Boston Basin TH
- **Drive from North Bend:** ~3.5 hrs to Marblemount, then ~45 min up Cascade River Rd
- **Permits:** NOCA wilderness permit; Boston Basin cross-country zone permit if bivying
- **SNOTEL:** Thunder Basin (`817:WA:SNTL`, 4,310 ft) — JSON has this correct

## Crux / hazards
- **Primary spring crux:** Sahale-Boston Col descent/traverse onto Boston Glacier can be impassable steep side-hill snow in May/early June (confirmed on [2026-05-04](buckner/2026-05-04-attempt.md)).
- **Sharkfin Col technicality:** right-side couloir + rappel transition can be exposed and complex; cornice and rockfall increase as melt progresses.
- **Boston Glacier travel:** crevasse hazard is substantial; rope team and glacier systems are mandatory.
- **N Face timing:** even if the face is in, approach hazards can still make the objective a no-go.
- **SW-face variant hazards:** steep snow with potential isolated wind slab; SE-bowl exit/reascent efficiency depends on overnight refreeze.

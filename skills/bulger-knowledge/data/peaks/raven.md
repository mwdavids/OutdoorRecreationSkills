# Raven Ridge

**Bulger #49 · 8590 ft · Prom 1130 ft**

## Status
Unclimbed (as of May 2026).

## Personal Context
Raven Ridge is a classic Bulger objective on the dry side of the Cascades. Located in the Chelan Sawtooth Wilderness, it sits among other high peaks and makes a great snow climb in late May/early June. Standard route is well-established via Libby Lake Trail. Best in Sept/Oct for larch color and stable conditions.

## Summary
Raven Ridge (8590 ft) sits on the drier side of the Cascades in the Chelan Sawtooth Wilderness. It rises 1130 ft above its connecting saddle with Star Peak. Views from the summit include Mount Bigelow to the south and Hoodoo Peak to the north. The peak has historical mystique: the true high point is the eastern summit (8572 ft), though the USGS Martin Peak Quadrangle incorrectly labels the western summit (8580 ft) as highest. Standard route ascends via the scenic Libby Lake Trail, then a loose Class 2+ gully to gain the ridge, followed by easy scramble along the ridge to the summit block (Class 3 via small tunnel under boulders). Total route is ~13 mi RT with ~4200 ft gain. Excellent snow climb in May–June.

## Best-Season Window
**Optimal:** Late September through early October (larch color, dry conditions, stable weather).

**May–June:** Great snow climb window, though road to trailhead may not be fully melted by late May. Ice axe, snowshoes, and warm clothing required through mid-June; crampons recommended if gully holds snow.

**July–August:** Feasible but avoid August if forest fires are active.

**Overall:** May–October are all viable; best is Sept–Oct due to larch color and trail conditions.

## Standard Route
**Libby Lake Trail approach, then loose gully to ridge, then ridge scramble.** Class 3 total. Distance: 13 mi RT. Elevation gain: ~4200 ft. Time: 8–10 hrs typical.

### Approach
**Trailhead:** Libby Lake Trailhead accessed via Libby Creek Road from Pateros (see Access Notes for full directions).

**Libby Lake Trail:** Excellent maintained trail, 5.5 mi and 3185 ft gain from trailhead (4433 ft) to Libby Lake (7618 ft). Route ascends with nice switchbacks to a gap between Mission Peak and Hoodoo Peak ridge (~5600 ft), then flattens briefly. Rounds right of Hoodoo's northeast ridge, climbs 400 ft on ridge, then traverses southeast side at steep slope. Crosses North Libby Creek at 5950 ft and again at 6400 ft. At 7200 ft, past Hoodoo Tarn (small cabin marks the spot), reaches steep 400 ft switchback section to lake. Pass small wooden drain structure at southeast corner of Libby Lake.

### Summit Route
**From Libby Lake:** Locate broad steep gully heading south through cliffs above lake. This gully is loose, dangerous, and exposed—only 900 ft but requires care and focus.

**Gully ascent (Class 2+):** Ascend talus cone for 200 ft to 7800 ft where gully narrows. Talus cone has some rolling boulders; stay aware. Enter gully proper and stay on left side, utilizing solid rock wall as handholds. Loose footing but rock wall provides excellent aid. At 8200 ft, climb out of gully to the left, gain the rib, and scramble up more solid rock to ridge crest at 8473 ft.

**Ridge traverse and summit (Class 3):** From crest, cross to south side a few feet and traverse east along ridge for ~0.5 mi. Summit confusion history: western peak (8580 ft labeled) was historically thought highest, but eastern summit (8572 ft labeled) is the true high point—USGS quad is incorrect. Traverse reaches a summit block requiring descent to 8380 ft saddle just before final ascent. Small peak "Corax" sits just west of saddle (easily ascendable if time permits).

**Summit block:** At first appears 4th–5th class, but small tunnel under large boulders on southwest side crosses through and leads to Class 3 scramble up final 20 ft to highest boulder. Alternatively, several 4th class options available. Summit sits perched above north face—dramatic exposure looking down.

### Descent
Retrace steps carefully back to Libby Lake. **Critical:** Use extreme care in gully descent to avoid dislodging rocks onto party members or other climbers below. Gully is extremely loose; falling rocks are a serious hazard.

## Equipment
**Essential:**
- Hiking boots with good ankle support and grippy soles (gully rock is loose and steep)
- Daypack (30–40L)
- Water (2–3 L capacity; water sources on trail and at Libby Lake)
- Snacks/meals (13 mi day, high elevation gain)
- Map and compass (Libby Lake Trail is well-marked, but gully route-finding helps with map)

**Early season (May–mid-June):**
- Ice axe (for self-arrest practice and steep snow)
- Microspikes or crampons (if gully holds snow)
- Snowshoes (if deep snow on trail below 6000 ft)
- Warm insulating layers and hat
- Gloves

**All seasons:**
- Insulating layer and wind layer (weather changes rapidly)
- Sun protection: sunscreen SPF 30+, sunglasses, hat
- Headlamp (long day; safety for descent in dim light)

## Alternates / Variations

**Hoodoo Peak from Libby Lake:** Easy addon (1300 ft gain, 2 mi extra). From the cabin at 7200 ft on Libby Lake Trail, scramble south ridge to Hoodoo summit. Good views of Raven Ridge and surrounding country.

**Ridge traverse: Hoodoo to Raven Ridge (or reverse):** Direct ridge traverse between peaks provides spectacular exposure and technical scrambling. Not recommended for inexperienced parties. Class 4 with intense exposure on knife-edged ridge. Notable crux: 5.7 overhanging boulder problem partway through (short parties may need a boost; 30 ft rappel sling available for opposite direction). Experienced scramblers can do it without ropes but requires confidence on exposed 4th class. Plan at least 2 hrs for traverse. Dropping lower on west side of ridge can bypass boulder problem but terrain is very loose and less fun. Excellent adventure for experienced alpinists.

**Corax Peak:** Small peak west of summit saddle at 8380 ft. Easy addon if time permits; obvious scramble from ridge traverse.

## Tactical Notes
**Start early:** Begin hike well before dawn to complete 13 mi and 4200 ft in daylight. Arrive at trailhead before 5 AM to start hike by 6 AM.

**Gully hazards:** The ascent gully is loose, exposed, and dangerous. Falling rocks are a serious hazard to yourself and others. **On descent, extreme caution required**—move slowly, communicate with party members and other parties in the gully, and avoid dislodging rocks. Small rocks cascade and gain speed quickly. This is the most serious hazard on the peak.

**Party size:** 2–4 optimal. Larger parties create additional rockfall hazard in gully. Solo possible for experienced scramblers but not recommended given gully danger.

**Water:** Water available on Libby Lake Trail and at Libby Lake. Hoodoo Tarn is often dry by mid-season.

**Weather:** Afternoon thunderstorms possible in summer. Turn around if weather deteriorates. Exposed summit block has lightning risk—descend if storm threatens.

**Fire conditions:** Avoid climbing in August during active fire season if smoke is present.

**Larch color:** Late Sept–early Oct, gold larch trees along Libby Lake Trail are spectacular. This window offers the best combination of weather stability and visual beauty.

**Rock scrambling skill:** This is not a casual hike. Class 2+ gully and Class 3 summit block require solid scrambling ability. Inexperienced parties should consider the ridge traverse to Hoodoo or alternative peaks.

**Return time target:** Aim to be back at trailhead by 6–7 PM to allow safety margin for descent in good light.

## Combinations

**Hoodoo Peak same-day combo:** Feasible and popular. Adds 1300 ft gain and ~2 mi to the 13 mi RT total. Can be done via the south ridge approach from the cabin at 7200 ft on Libby Lake Trail, or via the direct ridge traverse (more technical, see Alternates). Total trip would be ~15 mi and ~5500 ft gain; 10–12 hrs for fast parties. This is a legitimate same-day combo for experienced scramblers.

**Raven Ridge + Hoodoo ridge traverse:** For experienced alpinists only. Spectacular exposure, Class 4 difficulties, 5.7 boulder crux. Not a casual combo.

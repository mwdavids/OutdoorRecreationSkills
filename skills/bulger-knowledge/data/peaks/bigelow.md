## Summary
Mount Bigelow is one of the more rugged Bulger peaks in the Lake Chelan-Sawtooth Wilderness, an area that typically sees less traffic than Seattle-favored objectives in the Enchantments and North Cascades. That relative obscurity is part of the appeal: fewer crowds, strong scenery, and several worthwhile Top 100 peaks in the same zone.

Within the Lake Chelan-Sawtooth Wilderness Top 100 group (Star, Oval, Martin, Switchback, Raven Ridge, Hoodoo, Courtney, Reynolds, and Bigelow), Bigelow stands out for terrain character. Its north side forms a broad amphitheater roughly half a mile across, with steep cliff bands and a small rock glacier. The long East Wall is a major barrier from Upper Eagle Lakes, with mostly Class 5 or unclimbable cliff, but one weakness near the north end allows an exposed Class 3 summit finish. The gentlest line is from the west via talus slopes above Summit Trail, usually easier but longer.

## Best-Season Window
Primary window is mid-summer through fall, when Eagle Lake Trail access is clear and the upper mountain is mostly snow-free talus and ledges. Early season can add snow on the exposed upper finish from Upper Eagle Lake and on ridge traverses.

Late September and early October can be especially beautiful in the Sawtooths when the larches turn, so that shoulder-season window is worth noting if early storms have not started.

Candidate seasons: summer, fall

## Standard Route
Most practical day-trip strategies use Eagle Lakes access from the Crater Creek Trailhead and then choose either:
1) a west-slope finish via Horsehead Pass (easier, longer), or
2) a more direct Upper Eagle Lake line with an exposed Class 3 finish.

Typical difficulty: Class 2 to exposed Class 3 (depending on finish). Strong parties can do Bigelow car-to-car in a day.

### Approach
From the Crater Creek Trailhead (4740 ft), follow Eagle Lake Trail No. 431 (signed from the Crater Creek Trail No. 416 start) for about 5 miles to the main fork:

- Left fork: continue past Lower Eagle Lake (6490 ft) to Horsehead Pass (about 7520+ ft), cross Sawtooth Ridge, then climb Bigelow by the easier west slopes.
- Right fork: continue to Upper Eagle Lake (7110 ft), then angle northwest toward Bigelow, staying right of the major rock wall until its upper end.

Alternative approach: from Crater Creek Trail northeast of Bigelow, gain East Ridge by ascending the benign north slopes of Point 8356, then traverse west on the East Ridge to the summit.

### Summit Route
Upper Eagle Lake direct line: from the lake, trend northwest and keep right of the prominent East Wall. Near the upper end of that wall, a weakness allows an exposed Class 3 finish to the summit.

West-slope line (via Horsehead Pass): generally easier and less technical, mostly talus travel from the west side above Summit Trail.

East Ridge variation (from Point 8356): rugged but manageable on the crest or south side. Do not drift to the northwest side of Point 8356, where terrain drops into steep to overhanging cliffs feeding the north amphitheater.

### Descent
Most parties reverse their ascent route. A useful option is to combine routes into a loop: climb via Eagle Lakes/Upper Eagle Lake and return via Crater Creek-side trail network, or vice versa. The Crater Creek-side variation is a couple miles shorter and works well as a scenic return leg when conditions are stable.

## Access Notes & Permits
Primary access for Lake Chelan-Sawtooth Wilderness objectives is from the Methow Valley side.

To reach Crater Creek Trailhead:
- Drive SR-153 to Gold Creek Road about 5 miles south of Carlton.
- Follow Gold Creek Road 6.9 miles to Crater Creek Road No. 3109.
- Continue 4.8 miles on Road 3109 to the trailhead at about 4740 ft.

Lake Chelan approach option: Bigelow can also be approached from the lake via Prince Creek Trail No. 1255 after boat drop-off, though this is usually chosen for broader traverses rather than a Bigelow-only climb.

## Tactical Notes
- The key navigation decision is at the Eagle Lakes fork: west slopes for easier terrain, Upper Eagle Lake for a more direct but more exposed finish.
- The East Wall above Upper Eagle Lake is mostly technical cliff; stay on the known weakness near its north end for Class 3 access.
- On East Ridge variants, remain on the crest or south side near Point 8356; northwest-facing terrain becomes cliffed and can be overhung.
- Bigelow is reasonable as a car-to-car day objective for fast parties, but build in extra time for routefinding on upper talus and ledges.

## Combinations
Natural same-zone pairings and sequence planning in the Lake Chelan-Sawtooth Wilderness include Star, Oval, Martin, Switchback, Raven Ridge, Hoodoo, Courtney, and Reynolds. Bigelow also fits into larger wilderness traverses where a Lake Chelan water-taxi drop at Prince Creek is already part of the itinerary.

# Mount Logan (9,087 ft / lidar 9,101.7 ft, Bulger #13)

## Personal status
- **Climbed:** no
- **Attempts:** none
- **Style preference:** Realistically a **3-4 day trip**, not a c2c day push. Logan is one of the deepest peaks in the North Cascades — the standard Fremont Glacier route is ~40+ mi RT with ~10,000 ft gain from Colonial Creek. Frequently linked with **Goode, Storm King, Black, or Buckner** for trip efficiency (same Park Creek / Fisher Creek basins). If pairing with another Bulger, plan the multi-peak link.

## Summary
The fourth-highest peak in North Cascades National Park and the highest non-volcanic peak between Glacier and Goode. Sits on the Cascade Crest in the **Goode-Logan-Black area** of the Central North Cascades; Park / Bridge Creeks drain east to Lake Chelan, Thunder / Fisher Creeks drain west to the Skagit. Coords 48.5366 N, 120.9526 W. **PeakBagger PID 1828.** Bulger #13, WA Top 100 #14, prom 1,518 ft, county Skagit. Carries three glaciers — **Fremont (NW), Banded (E), Douglas (S)** — all three are used as access lines. FA: Lage Wernstedt (Forest Service surveyor), July 1926. Subpeak: Logan Middle Peak (8,981 ft) west of the true summit.

Any route to the top finishes on **class 3-4 rock** at the summit block; the bulk of the work is the **approach** and **glacier travel**. Skagit gneiss is solid where it matters — SummitPost author called the summit ridge "some of the best alpine scrambling I have ever had." The route's reputation comes from how far in it is — Logan is far from any road, period.

## Best-season window
- **Mid-July through August** — prime climbing window for all three routes. Bergschrunds passable, glacier crevasses well-bridged, snow consolidated, talus mostly clear.
- **Late June – early July:** still in but snow can be deep and unconsolidated, avalanche debris common in Thunder Basin even in July (SummitPost 2011 TR). Bergschrund on Banded is best when full.
- **Early September:** **Banded Glacier route closes out** as the bergschrund opens. Mountaineers note: "Not recommended in late season. A large bergschrund develops at the top of the glacier." Fremont still goes (different terrain).
- **Late September – October:** Fremont Glacier route can still go with care (PeakBagger has an Oct 2 2022 TR with photos showing thin Hogsback conditions). Increasing loose rock, ice, exposure.
- **Spring (April-June):** **ski-mountaineering window** — Douglas and Banded both have skiable terrain. Very remote multi-day spring ski objective; few parties attempt it.

## Standard route — Fremont Glacier (NW side, Class 3-4 + glacier)
Considered the standard by most. Long approach, glacier travel, class 4 summit ridge. SummitPost author calls it a "5-star route" but admits "it will not be easy and usually takes between 3 and 4 days to complete."

### Approach: Colonial Creek Campground (SR 20 MP 130)
The standard TH. **West of the SR 20 seasonal closure — accessible year-round from west side.**

1. **Colonial Creek Campground (1,200 ft)** → Thunder Creek Trail south. Two parking options: east end of campground near restrooms, or pullout across SR 20 (+¼ mi each way). No fee.
2. **First 9 mi**: mostly flat and forested through Thunder Creek valley. Bridge crossing at 1.5 mi. **Tricouni Camp at 7 mi.** Then switchbacks up to views of Fisher Creek.
3. **"Junction" (~9 mi)**: halfway point to Park Creek Pass. Trail junction with Fisher Creek Trail (left fork goes to Easy Pass / Douglas Glacier variation; stay right for Fremont). Camp here possible.
4. **After Junction:** ~1,000 ft elevation loss into the valley, then double-log bridge, then climb again. **Skagit Queen Camp** (12-13 mi) or **Thunder Basin / Horse Stock Camp** (~15 mi) are the standard day-1 stopping points.
5. **Past Skagit Queen:** old mining cabin and pipeline ruins. Trail crosses Thunder Creek with no bridge — usually wadeable, sometimes a log crossing 100 ft upstream of the standard ford.
6. **Thunder Basin (~16-17 mi):** flowers, brush, mountains finally come into view. Logan and Buckner visible.
7. **Switchbacks up toward Park Creek Pass.** At the top, before reaching the pass proper, **leave the trail at the boulder field** and start heading north-east up into the bowl below the SW flank of Logan. (Optional: tag Park Creek Pass for the view, then double back.)
8. **Climb up and east through the bowl,** aiming for a set of trees on the minor ridge to the north. Don't gain too much elevation — stay near treeline. Cross the ridge.
9. **Fremont Basin:** navigate north, drifting east. **Camp ~7,900 ft near Point 8248** on the edge of the Fremont Glacier — flat spots, water available.

### Ascent: Fremont Basin camp → summit (~1,200 ft)
1. **Rope up** at the edge of the Fremont Glacier. Head NE. In poor visibility, keep right (close to rock walls).
2. **The Hogsback** — distinctive arcing snow/ice formation. **Take the higher (left) branch.** If you reach a rock wall without climbing the Hogsback, you're off route.
3. **Above the Hogsback** the glacier ends. Drop rope (may be able to drop crampons depending on season).
4. **Traverse north (left)** across a couple of dicey spots — care needed.
5. **Class 3-4 scramble** off the west face onto the **south ridge**. Exposed but solid rock with good holds.
6. **South ridge to summit:** mostly horizontal scrambling with a few fun moves, then the final pull to the summit. Class 3-4 throughout.

### Descent
Reverse the route. The Hogsback section deserves attention on the way down.

### Stats (Fremont Glacier from Colonial Creek)
- **~40+ mi RT, ~10,000 ft gain.** 3-4 days standard.
- **Class 3-4 + glacier travel.** Full glacier kit (rope, harness, axe, crampons, pickets, crevasse rescue).
- **Mountaineers party limit: 6** (NOCA backcountry rule for this cross-country zone).

### Stehekin variation
**Cuts ~6 mi each way** off the Colonial Creek approach. Ferry-dependent and more expensive but a real option:
- Lady of the Lake from Chelan → Stehekin Landing → shuttle bus to end of Stehekin River Road (~11 mi up the road from Stehekin Landing; was 13.5 mi pre-2003 floods).
- Bike or walk the closed road up to **Park Creek Trailhead** (currently ~18 mi from the landing).
- **Park Creek Trail** 8.5 mi to Park Creek Pass — gradual, mostly forested, steep first and last miles. Camps at 2 and 5 mi from trailhead.
- From Park Creek Pass: leave trail, head NE, same as steps 8-9 above.

## Alternate route — Banded Glacier (E side, Class 4 + glacier)
**Less common, more committing.** Mountaineers Basic Alpine Climb. Same Colonial Creek TH but via **Fisher Creek Trail**.

### Approach (Mountaineers beta)
1. Colonial Creek → Thunder Creek Trail 9 mi → **left fork onto Fisher Creek Trail** at Junction.
2. ~3 mi on Fisher Creek Trail to just past a **good log bridge with handrail** over the stream draining the 5,160 ft lake.
3. **Leave trail, ascend the drainage** to camp at the lake (5,160 ft). Stay close to stream on ascent into the basin.

### Ascent
1. From camp, ascend **SSE to a low point on the ridge** — possibly Christmas Tree Col. May need to rope up for steep snow or a short glacier segment.
2. From the col, **drop down onto the Banded Glacier.** Ascend to the **Banded-Douglas Col**.
3. Climb snow to summit area. **Large bergschrund at top of glacier traversed on the left.**
4. Snow and rock to summit block. **Class 4 climbing** to reach the small summit.

### Stats (Banded Glacier)
- **42 mi RT.** 3-4 days. Stronger parties only for 3-day trips.
- **Trip profile** (Mountaineers): TH→Camp 10 h / 3,100 ft; Camp→Summit 6-8 h; descent 4-5 h to camp, 7-9 h camp→TH.
- **Difficulty:** Technical 3, Strenuous 5. Basic Glacier + Basic Experience Climb.
- **Window:** June, July, August only. **Bergschrund develops late season** and route gets significantly more dangerous (loose rock, ice, exposure).

## Alternate route — Douglas Glacier (S side, shortest but committing)
**The shortest approach to Logan**, but technically more challenging — not the most popular for that reason.

### Approach: Easy Pass TH (SR 20 ~MP 151)
- **Easy Pass TH (~3,700 ft)** is on SR 20 east of the typical winter closure (Silver Star → Ross Dam, late-Nov to ~late-May). In shoulder season this TH is **inaccessible from the west** until SR 20 reopens, or requires the long east-side drive via Winthrop.
- Easy Pass Trail ~3.5 mi to **Easy Pass (~6,500 ft)** → descend into **Fisher Creek basin** → traverse into the upper Fisher Creek / North Fork Bridge Creek drainage.
- Alternate access: at the 10 mi point on the Thunder Creek/Fisher Creek trail junction, take the left fork to Easy Pass area.

### Ascent
- Up the **Douglas Glacier** on the south side of the peak → Banded-Douglas Col → summit block via the same finish as Banded.

### Stats
- Total distance shorter than Fremont or Banded but **more technical** — steeper glacier, more crevasse hazard, less margin.

## Access notes / red tape
- **NOCA backcountry permit** required for all overnight camping. Available at **Marblemount Ranger Station** in person, or via NOCA's online reservation system in season.
- **SR 20 closure:** Silver Star → Ross Dam closed late-Nov to ~late-May. **Colonial Creek TH is west of the closure (year-round access).** Easy Pass TH is east of the closure (Douglas Glacier variation effectively summer-only via west approach).
- **Cascade River Road** is irrelevant to Logan (that's the Boston/Cascade Pass approach, different drainage).
- **Stehekin access:** Lady of the Lake ferry from Chelan (`ladyofthelake.com`), or Chelan Seaplanes. Stehekin shuttle bus runs the Stehekin River Road (~$5 per person). Road ends ~11 mi up the valley.
- **Mountaineers/NPS party limit:** 6 in this cross-country zone.
- **Recommended map:** Green Trails *North Cascades National Park, WA No. 16SX*. Guidebook: Beckey *Cascade Alpine Guide Vol. 3 (Rainy Pass to Fraser River)*.

## Combinations
- **+ Goode**: Goode's SW Route shares the lower Park Creek approach. Common 5-7 day trip linking both peaks from the Stehekin / Cascade Pass side. Goode + Logan from Park Creek Pass area is a classic Bulger double.
- **+ Storm King**: Storm King's SW Route shares the Park Creek Camp basecamp with Logan-from-Stehekin. Can stack Logan via Fremont with Storm King for a 4-5 day double Bulger trip.
- **+ Buckner / Boston**: less natural pairing — different basins (Buckner is north over Boston Glacier, accessed from Cascade Pass / Eldorado side). Mega-traverses occasionally link them but it's a serious undertaking.
- **+ Black, Goode, Storm King mega-link**: the "Goode-Logan-Black area" Bulger sweep is a multi-week alpine expedition for very strong parties.

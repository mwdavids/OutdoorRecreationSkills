## Summary
Iconic North Cascades alpine rock peak in Boston Basin, sharing the trailhead and basin with Buckner / Boston / Sahale routes. The **West Ridge (5.6)** is one of [Fifty Classic Climbs of North America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fifty_Classic_Climbs_of_North_America) — moderate, long, alpine, and **crowded by Cascades standards**. The climbing itself is well within reach of competent alpine rock parties; the real obstacles are the Boston Basin permit quota, the moat at the top of the south snowfield, and the time pressure of a long ridge with a fiddly mixed descent.

## Best-season window
- **Mid-July through early September:** standard window. Snowfield approach consolidated, moat manageable, rock dry. JSON `best_seasons: ["summer"]` is right.
- **Late July – mid August:** Cat Scratch gully variant becomes increasingly the norm as the snow gully melts out. Late-season parties expect the rock variant of the gully.
- **Early summer (June – early July):** snow gully is the standard line to the ridge — different gear assumption (more snow climbing, less rock climbing on the gully). Moat may be filled.
- **Avoid:** wet weather (Boston Basin meadows + the slabby approach + sopping rock = miserable and slow). Watch for refreeze on descent if a late summit makes you ice-out the snowfield.
- **Winter ascents exist** (Eric Gilbertson + Duncan, Jan 22–23 2022 = first/early winter ascent of the West Ridge) — out of scope for a standard objective; reference link in Sources.

## Standard route — West Ridge (5.6, 50 Classics)
From Boston Basin camp (~6,400 ft): scramble wet slabs to base of south snowfield → cramponed snow climb up to the moat at top → cross moat → climb the **Cat Scratch gully** system (rightmost gully, ~3 pitches, low 5th max on bottom pitch) → 3rd class to crest of West Ridge → long ridge climbing on solid rock with kiwi-coil + short pitches + horn-wraps (mostly 4th and low 5th, big ledges) → crux **knife-edge → 5.6 step** near top → false summit → **15 ft downclimb + traverse to true summit**.

- **Grade:** 5.6 alpine rock; one true 5.6 step; rest is 4th + low 5th
- **Length:** long ridge — count on a full long day even from camp
- **Snow vs rock balance:** late-season = rock-dominant via Cat Scratch; early-season = snow-dominant via the original snow gully
- **The "south glacier":** not actually a glacier — no real crevasses, no need for rope team / pickets / glacier rescue gear

## Alternate routes
- **East Ridge:** seen in use by other parties from the summit; less commonly the standard. Beta not yet researched.
- **North Face / North Ridge / NW Face:** harder, longer, sees less traffic. Out of scope unless specifically targeting them.

## Access & logistics
- **Trailhead:** Boston Basin TH on **Cascade River Road**. Note: as of NPS NOCA alert dated 2025-09-30, **Cascade River Road is closed at MP 20 (Eldorado), 2 mi short of the Boston Basin TH** at MP 22. Foot/bike traffic permitted past gate. **Pre-trip: check NPS NOCA road status — this gate may move further down-road if Boston Creek washout repairs continue (per Eric Gilbertson 2024-02 reporting).**
- **Drive from North Bend:** ~2.5–3 hrs (I-90 → I-405 → I-5 → SR 20 → Cascade River Rd to gate).
- **Permits:**
  - **Boston Basin Cross-Country Zone permit** required for camping; **quota = 6 groups/night**, very competitive in summer. Pick up at Wilderness Information Center in Marblemount or via NPS reservation system.
  - C2C parties don't need a camping permit but still must register at the trailhead.
- **Approach distance/gain (from MP 22 trailhead, when road is open):** ~3-4 mi, ~4,500 ft to camp at 6,400 ft. Add ~2 mi each way for current MP 20 gate.
- **NWAC zone (per JSON):** `cascade-west-central`
- **SNOTEL (per JSON):** `817:WA:SNTL` — proxy only; standard summer trip doesn't need snowpack monitoring beyond confirming the south snowfield is in.
- **Style options:** overnight from Boston Basin (most common); C2C from car for fast/competent parties (long day from current gate).

## Crux / hazards
- **Time management on the long ridge** is the real crux for non-guided parties. Pitching the whole ridge will leave you benighted. The standard practice (and what Eric used) is **kiwi-coil + short pitches + weave around horns + 1-2 pieces**, belaying when not in sight/sound.
- **Moat at top of south snowfield** is the consistent transition crux — variable per year/week. Verify before committing.
- **Refreeze on descent:** late summit → icy snowfield on the way down. Plan to build a nut anchor + ice-axe anchor at the top of the snow if late.
- **Crowding + dropped rocks** from parties above on Cat Scratch — the rap line is climbers' left of the climbed gully precisely to avoid this.
- **Stolen rap anchors at the base** of the gully — verified missing in 2018 (Eric). Carry enough rack to build one at the moat if needed.
- **The 15 ft downclimb to the true summit** is non-trivial and required for a Bulger-list summit. Skipping it = not summitted.

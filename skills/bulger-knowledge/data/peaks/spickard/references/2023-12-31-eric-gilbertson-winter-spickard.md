# Eric Gilbertson + Nick — Winter Spickard (south face from Perry Creek), Dec 31 2023

**Source:** https://www.countryhighpoints.com/winter-hard-mox-and-spickard/
**Author:** Eric Gilbertson
**Posted:** 2024-01-05
**Trip dates:** 2023-12-27 through 2024-01-02 (Spickard summit day = 2023-12-31)
**Party:** Eric Gilbertson, Nick
**Outcome:** Second Winter Ascent of Spickard. (Trip's primary objective was Hard Mox = First Winter Ascent on 2023-12-29. Spickard was the bonus add-on.)

## Why preserved in full
Only known detailed beta for a US-only winter approach to Spickard. Documents:
1. The full Ross Lake / Perry Creek approach as a winter logistical solution for any of the southern Chilliwack peaks (Spickard, Hard Mox).
2. The Solitude-Spickard glacier south-side route — essentially the standard south face reached from the south side instead of from Lake Ouzel.
3. Confirmation that the south face is the easiest winter line; NE ridge (Stu Johnson + Max Bond, ~Dec 2022, 1st Winter Ascent) "looked way more difficult."

## Approach (Dec 27-28)

**The approach problem:** Hard Mox / Spickard standard summer approach via Depot Creek requires crossing into Canada. Eric's rules: ascents must be entirely legal on US side. Ross Dam TH and Hannegan Pass TH are the two nearest US-side options (both ~28-30 mi one-way of trail + bushwhack). Hannegan road is groomed for skiing in winter, adding 5 mi. Ross Dam TH route requires getting up Ross Lake, where road access for the public has been washed out since Nov 2021.

**Solution: motorized inflatable zodiac on Ross Lake.**
- Carry boat + 5 hp propane motor + 5 gal propane tank + accessories down 0.6 mi trail from Ross Dam TH to Frontage Road, then 0.5 mi down Frontage Road to lake. Multi-trip carry: ~2 hrs.
- Inflate boat on shore (SUP hand pump faster than foot pump).
- Boat ~15 mi up Ross Lake at 5.7 mph (~2.5 hrs) to **Little Beaver**.
- Critical lake-level conditions: stumps emerge at low water; lake must be ice-free; wind must be calm.
- Custom yellow fog lights for night boating.
- Custom GPS track to follow deepest water and avoid stumps.

**Foot from Little Beaver:**
- Trail snow-free for first 3 mi (light hiking boots), then deep snow → switch to mountaineering boots + snowshoes.
- Reached **Perry Creek shelter** at sunset (Dec 27).

**Bushwhack up Perry Creek (Dec 28):**
- Ditch hiking boots in the cabin. Stay close to creek; only bushwhack around cliffs and waterfalls.
- "Sweet spot" of bad conditions: creek too high to walk slabs, snow not deep enough to cover them — required more side-bushwhacks than ideal. (Best conditions: low creek + dry slabs OR deep snow cover.)
- One slide-alder thicket required crawling up steep alder with mud below — dislocated shoulder hazard.
- Reach **old growth forest at 3,400 ft** — fast travel from there.
- **Camp at 4,500 ft** at edge of treeline above the talus field. (Eric chose 4,500 ft over higher 6,700 ft camp because of avy hazard on the approach to the higher camp.)
- Bushwhack took 9 hrs in this trip (vs 6 hrs in Feb 2023 with better snow coverage).

## Spickard summit day (Dec 31)

After Hard Mox on Dec 29 + a rest day Dec 30 (rain), Eric and Nick used Dec 31 for Spickard since it was the only other Bulger accessible from their 4,500 ft Perry Creek camp. Cols to reach Redoubt and Easy Mox were too crevassed / corniced.

**Route from camp:**
- Up at sunrise, moving by 6am. Snow had firmed up overnight with icy crust — good news for stability. Took turns breaking trail.
- Ascended **the leftmost (largest) of four north-trending snow gullies** to a **low-angle shoulder at 6,000 ft**.
- Traversed right to a big drainage, then up to **the toe of the Solitude-Spickard glacier**.
- Sun came out; great undercast views below.
- Met up with the standard Spickard route at this point.
- **Traversed right on a ledge between two cliff bands** to gain the south face proper.
- Climbed **low-angle slopes almost to the ridge**, then **traversed right below a steeper band**.
- Snow was stable with only thin unreactive wind slabs in isolated places.
- Below a small saddle, ditched snowshoes and switched to **ascent plates** (mini snowshoes that sandwich between crampon and boot).
- Marched up to the saddle, continued along the ridge until **blocked by gendarmes**.
- Found a **snow traverse on the north face** below the gendarmes; ditched ascent plates, got out ice tools.
- One by one traversed the **icy north-face section** to reach the **summit by noon**.
- Summit was sunny + windless — rare luxury for a winter summit.

**Descent:** Reversed route exactly. Traversed back across N face, downclimbed to ascent plates, packed up, cramponed back down south face. Recovered snowshoes, retraced track. Back at 4,500 ft camp before sunset.

## Key Spickard-specific takeaways

- **South face is the easiest winter line.** Eric explicitly contrasts it with the **NE ridge that Stu Johnson + Max Bond climbed (~Dec 2022)** — the NE ridge "looked way more difficult."
- **South face is the same in winter as in summer**, just reached from the south (Perry Creek glacier) instead of from the north (Lake Ouzel). The route on the upper mountain is identical from either side once you intersect at the south face.
- **Gendarmes block the ridge** — the bypass is a snow/ice traverse on the north face that requires ice tools but is short.
- **Gear for a winter ascent:** snowshoes, ascent plates, crampons, ice tools (whippets enough on the lower mountain, real tools for the N-face traverse), single rack of cams + nuts (probably overkill for Spickard alone).

## Key approach takeaways

- **Ross Lake must be entirely ice-free** — a half-mile of ice near Little Beaver killed the January 2023 attempt (had to bushwhack cliffs and packraft to bypass; took 6 hrs for 0.5 mi line-of-sight).
- **Lake level must be high enough to submerge stumps** if boating in marginal light.
- **Cold can kill the motor.** On the Jan 1 paddle-out the propane motor wouldn't start (likely cold-related); 15 mi of rowing took most of the day. Bring oars.
- **Multiple long-weekend windows are essential.** Even with all logistics solved, weather + snow + ice + wind + lake-level all have to align. Eric had two prior unsuccessful winter attempts (Jan 2023 ice; Feb 2023 storm bail mid-route).
- **Perry Creek bushwhack:** stay in/near creek, hit the old growth at 3,400 ft, then fast travel to 4,500 ft basin.

## Source confidence
- **Author reliability:** very high (Eric Gilbertson, a meticulous primary source).
- **Route-status verification:** verified in late Dec 2023 — beta is recent.
- **Generalizability:** the route description should hold for any winter / shoulder-season ascent from the south side, but the *approach* (boat on Ross Lake) is a one-of-a-kind logistical solution that depends on owning/borrowing a motorized inflatable. For a typical climber without that gear, the practical takeaway is: "winter Spickard from the US side is essentially impossible" — leaving the standard Depot Creek summer ascent as the only realistic option for most.

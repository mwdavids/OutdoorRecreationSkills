## Summary
A rugged set of rock fins arranged in an arc above a huge talus amphitheater, at the conjunctive head of Park Creek, North Fork Bridge Creek, and Thunder Creek. **PeakBagger lidar: 8,564.6 ft**; the 8,515 ft / 8,520 ft figures commonly cited in guidebooks correspond to the western subsummit — the true highpoint is the easternmost of two 8,520+ tops east of Spot 8515. Bulger #51 (tie). Located in **North Cascades National Park, Stephen Mather Wilderness, Goode-Logan-Black area** — *not* the Pasayten and *not* the Olympic Storm King. Coords 48.4913 N, 120.9228 W. PeakBagger PID 1833.

The peak holds two small glaciers (Wyeth on the NW outlier ridge to Logan; upper Goode Glacier on the SE/E up to the col with Goode), but the climb itself is a **loose Class 3–4 talus-and-ledge slog with a low Class 5 finish** on Skagit gneiss — same rock as Goode. Rock quality is fair-to-poor. FA: Forrest Farr & Art Winder, 1933. A North Face route exists (FA Dick Emerson & Walt Grove, 1978) but is rarely climbed.

**This is one of the farthest Top 100 peaks from a road**, especially since the upper Stehekin River Road was permanently closed past High Bridge after the 2003 floods. **Plan on three days minimum.** Long days, easy climbing, big approach — the peak's challenge is logistics, not technical difficulty.

## Best-season window
- **Mid-July through mid-September** — prime window. Talus mostly snow-free, gullies easy, no glacier issues on the standard route.
- **Late June – early July:** lingering snow between 7,400 ft and summit can cover the worst talus (a *good* thing here) but adds awkward steep snow traverses approaching the summit cirque. **Ice axe + crampons recommended even into August.**
- **Mid-September to first significant snowfall:** still in. Risk of early-season snow on summit ramparts adds Class 4 mixed and a worse rappel.
- **Late spring / winter:** upper ramparts not melted out enough for easy climbing — moves into committing snow-and-mixed territory. Not standard.

## Standard route — Southwest Route (Class 3-4 + low Class 5 finish)
The only route worth mentioning for a Bulger tick. Shares the approach with Goode SW Route below 7,400 ft, so frequently combined with Goode.

### Standard approach: Stehekin (Lady of the Lake + foot/bike up the road)
1. **Chelan → Stehekin Landing** via Lady of the Lake ferry (or Chelan Seaplanes). Shuttle from Stehekin Landing to High Bridge Camp (11 mi up Stehekin River Road; this is the current end of vehicle access).
2. **High Bridge → Bridge Creek Camp**: 5 mi on the now-decommissioned upper road (foot or bike).
3. **Bridge Creek Camp → Park Creek Camp (2,280 ft)**: another 2.5 mi. Park Creek Camp is the base camp for Storm King — multiple sites, unlikely to be crowded now that the road is gone.
4. **Park Creek Trail** climbs west off the campground (signed). Initial 1,000 ft of brushy woods, then traverses right above the creek. Passes a large cliff on the left, then crosses Park Creek (small camp on west side, ~2 mi from Park Creek Camp).
5. **Continue ~2 mi up the east side** to a large whitish-rock washout/rockslide. **About 5 minutes up-trail on the north side of this washout, a boot path branches off (small cairn) into the woods.** It's *before* any more small stream crossings.
6. **Boot path up the ridge** (~4,100 ft start) → burnt-out terrain from the Goode Mountain fire (ash, deadfall) for the first 1,000+ ft of gain. Trail fades; climb diagonally up climber's left through heather/meadows/game trails.
7. **~6,500 ft:** terrain steepens and goes rocky. Look for cairns marking the Class 2-3 climber's path up into the basin under Goode Mountain (~7,400 ft). **Excellent high camp here** — strenuous to reach in one day but worth it if combining with Goode.
8. **Traverse north** across snow and talus, bearing climber's left. Snow can linger into August; a few tricky moves where it does. After ~1 mile, arrive in the cirque between Storm King's west and east towers.
9. **Ascend the talus slope** to ~8,000 ft. **A gully with a horn-shaped rock at its top is the *wrong* gully — veer right into the correct gully**. (NWHikers TR confirms SummitPost's beta on this — the right gully is the standard.)
10. **Arrive at a notch**, scramble on the ledge on the north side: **exposed, Class 4 moves — many parties set up a short fixed line here.** Continue along the ledge (gets wider but more loose) until blocked by a cliff.
11. **Turn left, scramble Class 3 ~70 ft to a second notch**. Then a **15-ft crack at Class 4 to low Class 5** lands you at the rappel anchor. Class 3 for the few feet to the summit.

### Descent
- Slings around a boulder just below the summit. **Inspect and reinforce as necessary.**
- **Rappel east** off the summit back down to the north-side ledge. **Even with a 60 m rope you'll need to downclimb some Class 3** below the rappel.
- Reverse the ledge / second-notch / first-notch sequence and back down the SW basin and ridge.

### Stats (Mountaineers SW Route)
- **~27 mi RT, ~6,800 ft gain, high point 8,520 ft** (their measured-summit figure; lidar gives 8,564.6 ft).
- **3-day standard.** Mountaineers profile: ~4-5 h TH→camp + ~2,200 ft gain; ~7-9 h camp→summit + ~5,100 ft gain; reverse on day 3.

### Equipment
- Small alpine rock rack (cams to ~#1, a couple nuts, slings for the boulder anchor).
- 30 m rope minimum; **60 m preferred** for the rappel, but even 60 m doesn't cover the whole way → still a downclimb component.
- Ice axe + crampons (mandatory through August on snow traverses below the cirque).
- Helmets (loose rock everywhere).

## Alternate approaches (same Southwest Route on the peak itself)

### Cascade Pass from Marblemount (west-side ground approach)
**Recommended for the user given home base** — avoids the ferry shuffle. Adds ~5.5 mi of foot travel each way vs the Stehekin approach but eliminates ferry timing.

1. Drive Cascade River Road 22 mi to **Cascade Pass TH (3,660 ft)** at road's end.
2. Hike 4 mi up to **Cascade Pass (5,400 ft)** — the standard tourist trail.
3. Descend the east-side trail into Pelton Basin → Stehekin River drainage. **3 mi from pass → junction at 3,640 ft.** Take the right (lower) fork.
4. **~½ mi → Basin Creek (3,150 ft)** — old camp now flood-debris but usable.
5. **< 2 mi → Cottonwood Camp** (old end of the road; park-service tent, picnic table, outhouse, backpacker + horse sites).
6. **4 mi of flat road walking → Park Creek Camp.** From here, same as Stehekin approach steps 4-11 above.

- **Stats car → Park Creek Camp:** ~14 mi, +1,800 / -3,200 ft, 5-6 h.
- **Late-season access only** — Cascade River Road is gated mid-MP-21 in shoulder season (matches the Eldorado-TH-gate beta the user has been working with for Buckner/Forbidden); full road typically open by late June, gone again by late October.
- **Permits**: NOCA backcountry permit (Marblemount Ranger Station) — required as soon as you cross Cascade Pass. NW Forest Pass at the TH.

### Thunder Creek from SR 20 (long, not recommended)
- Drive Hwy 20 to Thunder Arm at Diablo Lake.
- **19 mi of "bone-bruising" trail south up Thunder Creek to Park Creek Pass**, then descend into Park Creek.
- ~2 days just to reach the base of the peak. SummitPost author explicitly does not recommend this — "a grind, I don't wish it on anyone."
- Use only if pairing with a Logan + Storm King + Goode mega-link traverse.

## Access notes / red tape
- **NOCA backcountry permit** for any overnight camping in the park. Available at the Marblemount Ranger Station (or online reservation system in season). Less competitive than Boston Basin.
- **NW Forest Pass** at Cascade Pass and Thunder Creek THs.
- **Stehekin access only by Lady of the Lake ferry, seaplane, or hike-in** (War Creek Trail from Twisp ~longest practical foot route). The upper Stehekin River Road past High Bridge is permanently closed to vehicles — foot/bike/stock only.

## Combinations
- **+ Goode Mountain SW Route**: shares the approach below 7,400 ft. Standard 4-day trip if doing both. Goode SW is the popular descent route off Goode NE Buttress, so a Goode NE Buttress climber camping in the high basin can swing Storm King the next morning as a moderate add-on.
- **+ Mt. Logan / Fremont Glacier**: per Mountaineers SW Route page — feasible as a 4- or 5-day trip linking the two via the basin.

## Key tactical notes
- **Burnt-out approach ridge** (Goode Mountain fire scar) — ash, deadfall, fading trail for the first 1,000+ ft above the Park Creek trail boot-path turnoff. Slow, dusty, can be hard to follow. The cairn at the turnoff and getting on the ridge nose early are the navigational keys.
- **Correct gully selection at ~8,000 ft** matters — the gully topped by a horn-shaped rock is wrong; the correct one is right of it. NWHikers TRs confirm parties have lost time on this.
- **Class 4 ledge on the north side** of the first notch is the most exposed move on the climb. A short fixed line is standard practice — bring 30 m of cord at minimum.
- **Rockfall** is the dominant objective hazard everywhere from the basin up. Helmets, small parties, don't follow a faster party up the gully.
- **Talus is endless.** SummitPost author calls it "Talus King." Plan for slow movement up to the cirque and slow descent back down.

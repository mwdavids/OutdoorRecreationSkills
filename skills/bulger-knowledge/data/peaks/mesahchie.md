# Mesahchie Peak (8,795 ft, Bulger #28)

## Personal status
- **Climbed:** no
- **Style preference:** TBD. Easternmost and tallest of the four Ragged Ridge Bulgers. The natural objective is the **full Ragged Ridge Traverse (Mesahchie + Katsuk + Kimtah + Cosho)** — see [katsuk.md](./katsuk.md), [kimtah.md](./kimtah.md), [cosho.md](./cosho.md). Standard practice is a 2–3 day camp-based traverse or a hard c2c sub-14-hour push for fit parties.

## Summary
Easternmost summit on Ragged Ridge, the long east-west spur in North Cascades NP that forms the divide between Granite Creek (north, draining to SR 20) and Fisher Creek (south, draining to Thunder Creek). PeakBagger lidar: **8,810 ft** (8,795 ft commonly cited). Prominence per LIDAR is **2,299 ft** (key col = Easy Pass), not the 2,731 ft in the JSON. Line parent: Black Peak. Coords 48.5785 N, 120.8805 W. PeakBagger PID 1822.

Of the four Ragged Ridge Bulgers, **Mesahchie is the most prominent and has the most striking north face** (snow/ice and ski terrain on the Mesahchie Glacier). The summit itself is reached by a fairly straightforward Class 3–4 south-side scramble; the real challenge of "doing the Ragged Ridge" is the loose, exposed traverse and 22-mile / 12,800-ft day if attempted car-to-car.

**Region 6: Ragged Ridge (highest point).** North Cascades NP, Stephen Mather Wilderness, Skagit County. NOCA cross-country zone (no permit needed for day use; backcountry permit required for any overnights in Fisher Basin or on the ridge).

## Best-season window
- **Late July through mid-September** — prime. Loose rock dry, snow patches small, traverse passable on dry ledges.
- **June – mid-July:** more snow on south-side traverse and in summit gullies. Steep snow on the south face gullies of Katsuk/Kimtah/Mesahchie can be runout-on-talus and is the standard early-season hazard.
- **Late April – early May (ski):** *spring ski variant from the north side* is in — see "Ski variant" below. Only Cosho is a ski summit; Mesahchie's south-side rock summit block is still a scramble.
- **October:** still in until first significant snow. Short days punish a c2c attempt.

## Access — Easy Pass TH (SR 20)
- **SR 20 closed Silver Star → Ross Dam late-November to ~late-May.** Easy Pass TH (MP ~151) is east of Ross Dam, on the seasonal closure side **but accessible from the east end of the closure** in late April/early May once the highway reopens — typically late April to mid-May depending on year. **In 2025 the reopening fell in late April**, enabling Galen Weld's partial ski traverse late April / early May.
- **Easy Pass TH coords ~48.6044 N, 120.7464 W, ~3,700 ft.**
- Trail to Easy Pass (~6,500 ft): **3.6 mi, ~2,800 ft gain.** Well-maintained, recently improved on the final 600 ft.
- **No NWFP fee in winter / off-season.** Self-issue NOCA cross-country permit required for overnight in Fisher Basin / on ridge.

## Standard summer route — South Side scramble (Class 3–4)
Standard approach for all four Ragged Ridge Bulgers; differences are which gully you climb to the ridge.

### Approach
1. **Easy Pass TH → Easy Pass (6,500 ft)** via standard trail, 3.6 mi.
2. **Leave the trail at Easy Pass before it begins descending into Fisher Creek.** Sidehill traverse **south side of Ragged Ridge** westward, mostly following the **6,600–6,800 ft contour**.
3. **~1 mi of sidehill** through gullies and buttresses → arrive at a small basin in the **6,700 ft range with a small rocky knob just above** (south of Mesahchie Peak). This basin contains the standard high camp; snow lingers into July.
4. **Camp at ~6,600 ft** in the basin south of Mesahchie. (Lemke 2014: ~5 h from TH to camp at relaxed pace.)
5. **Variation for c2c speed:** Kegan Buchhop (FKT 14h03 2023) recommends going directly to **Mesahchie–Katsuk col** from Easy Pass rather than camping — avoids bushwhacking entirely. GPX on FKT page.

### Mesahchie summit pitch
1. From the 6,600 ft camp basin, ascend **800 ft north** to a large flat bench at **7,400 ft** (great views east to Mesahchie and Katsuk).
2. The **final 250 ft to the bench is Class 4 either via ribs or gullies** — Lemke tried three different lines and all were "some tougher scrambling."
3. From the 7,400 ft bench, **traverse east a short distance** beneath the south face of Mesahchie. The **standard summit gully** is the obvious one heading up toward the summit block.
4. **Small waterfall greets you at the gully entry** — climb left of the water on a mix of granite and slate (granite is better quality). Continue up either side of the flowing water.
5. **At ~8,100 ft the gully forks**: take the **left fork** to the summit block. The right fork goes to a steeper snowfield with a scary runout.
6. **Avoid the steep summit snowfield by climbing the Class 3–4 rib just left of it**, separating it from another scary-looking gully further west.
7. **Final 200 ft to the 50-ft summit block:** follow the small moat on the left side of the snowfield as the rib narrows and becomes more difficult.
8. **Summit block climbed on its west side: Class 4.** Recent summit register placed 2004 in a plastic pill container.

### Descent
Reverse the route. Same gully back down to the 7,400 ft bench, then reverse the Class 4 ribs to the basin camp.

### Stats
- **From Easy Pass TH (c2c, Mesahchie only):** ~12 mi RT, ~6,000 ft gain. Achievable in a long day for fit parties.
- **As part of the full Ragged Ridge link-up:** 21.15 mi, 12,800 ft (Hardrath FKT route). ~14 hours for top-tier athletes; **17–20 hours realistic for strong parties.**

## Equipment
- **Helmet required** — loose rock throughout the south-side traverse and summit gullies.
- Approach shoes adequate; stiffer footwear better for the rib scrambling near the summit.
- **Light alpine rock rack optional** — most parties solo, but a 30-m rope + a couple of small cams adds confidence on the Class 4 summit block, especially on descent.
- Ice axe + light crampons if attempting before late July (snow in south-side gullies).
- **No glacier gear needed** for the summer south-side route. Glacier gear required only for the ski-traverse-from-north variant.

## Ski variant (Mesahchie Glacier, north side)
**Spring window: late April – mid-May**, immediately after SR 20 reopens. The Mesahchie Glacier on the north side holds excellent powder skiing, but **the Mesahchie summit block itself is not a ski summit** — only the glacier and ridge approaches. To tag the summit on skis you'd booter the standard south-side gully or the N Ridge. Most parties doing the spring ski variant **only tag Cosho** (the diminutive westernmost summit, easily reached by skis from the Kimtah Glacier saddle) and treat Mesahchie/Katsuk/Kimtah as glacier objectives only.

**Standard ski line (per Galen Weld 2025-05):**
1. Easy Pass TH → cross Granite Creek → continuous snow above ~4,000 ft → Easy Pass.
2. From Easy Pass, **steep traverse west** to access the Mesahchie Glacier (far above Fisher Creek).
3. Optional lap into the basin below Kitling Peak.
4. **Climb to col**, drop onto **northern aspects of Mesahchie Glacier** — "pretty great turns in spring powder."
5. Descend to **Panther Creek**, traverse west to Katsuk Glacier → Kimtah Glacier → camp at Ragged Ridge saddle below Cosho summit.

## Hazards
- **Loose rock** — defining hazard of the entire Ragged Ridge. South-side gullies funnel debris from parties above.
- **Steep snow in summit gullies** through July with runout onto talus/cliffs. Ice axe + crampons needed early-season.
- **Route-finding** on the south-side sidehill traverse — gullies that look passable from one elevation aren't from another. **Stay near 6,700–6,800 ft on the eastern half**, then **7,400 ft toward the Katsuk/Kimtah portion** of the traverse.
- **Long days, big commitment** — c2c is a true big-mountain day. **No bail options between Easy Pass and Cosho except retreating east.**

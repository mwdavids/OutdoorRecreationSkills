# Mount Spickard (8,979 ft, Bulger #16)

## Personal status
- **Climbed:** no
- **Attempts:** none
- **Style preference:** TBD — most likely summer alpine scramble from Depot Creek; ski mountaineering on Silver Glacier is theoretically viable but the access cost is enormous

## Summary
A remote, glaciated peak deep in the Chilliwack region of North Cascades NP, near the Canadian border. The climbing itself is moderate (steep snow + class 3-4 scramble on the south aspect), but **access is the dominant problem on every route**. The standard approach crosses into the US from southern BC via Depot Creek — a rough logging-road drive, an unmaintained climbers' trail, and a fixed-rope waterfall scramble. US-only approaches (Hannegan Pass / Chilliwack River, or boat-access via Ross Lake) are dramatically longer and effectively turn Spickard into a multi-day commitment.

## Best-season window
- **Mid-July through mid-September:** standard window for the Depot Creek approach. Snow consolidated on south face, glacier reasonably stable, climbers' trail mostly snow-free, Depot Creek waterfall fixed rope usable. The JSON `best_seasons: ["spring","summer"]` is right but "summer" is the realistic default — spring shifts the access problem from bushwhack to snowpack and approach-trail unfindability.
- **Early summer (June–early July):** more snow lower down can ease the bushwhack but Depot Creek waterfall is sketchier with high flow; scree gullies covered with snow help the upper mountain.
- **Winter:** climbed at least twice (Stu Johnson & Max Bond NE ridge ~Dec 2022; Eric Gilbertson & Nick south-face Dec 31 2023). Requires a fundamentally different US-only approach (Ross Lake boat → Perry Creek bushwhack → Solitude-Spickard glacier S side). Multi-day expedition with very few feasible weather windows.
- **Avoid:** any window with active rain in the Chilliwack zone — the Depot Creek climbers' trail and waterfall scramble become unsafe quickly, and the south face's small unreactive wind slabs can become reactive.

## Approach options

Three fundamentally different access modes; choice drives almost everything else about the trip.

- **Depot Creek from BC (standard):** Drive Chilliwack Lake Road to south end, then onto rough logging roads near the US border. Bushwhack and unmaintained climbers' trail up Depot Creek (one fixed-rope scramble next to a big waterfall) to Lake Ouzel basecamp at the foot of Spickard / Redoubt / Mox group. Border crossing required. Standard approach for ~all summer ascents and the only feasible day-to-summit strategy from camp.
- **Hannegan Pass → Chilliwack River → Bear Creek (US-only summer):** ~28+ mi one-way through Hannegan Pass, down to the Chilliwack River trail, then bushwhack up Bear Creek to gain the Chilliwack peaks region. Avoids border crossing but is roughly twice the mileage and requires multi-day. Rarely used for Spickard specifically — better suited to climbers tagging multiple Chilliwack peaks who can't cross into Canada. *(general knowledge — not source-verified for Spickard specifically; Eric Gilbertson scouted this approach for Hard Mox.)*
- **Ross Dam → Ross Lake → Perry Creek (US-only winter / shoulder-season):** Carry motorized zodiac (or packraft) down 0.6 mi from Ross Dam TH to Frontage Road, motor ~15 mi up Ross Lake to Little Beaver, hike + bushwhack up Perry Creek to a high basin (~4,500 ft) at the foot of the Solitude-Spickard glacier on Spickard's south side. Verified by Eric Gilbertson (Dec 2023, 2nd Winter Ascent). Multi-day. Boat-on-Ross-Lake is the binding logistical constraint and only feasible when the lake is ice-free and winds are calm.

## Standard route — South face from Lake Ouzel (via Depot Creek)
From Lake Ouzel basecamp: ascend toward the col leading to Silver Lake, then cut right before reaching the col. Climb steep snow slopes to gain the south ridge of Spickard, cross a small notch onto the east-face snowfield, traverse a few hundred feet, then climb a gully back up to the south ridge. Roughly follow the ridge southwest, traversing below several false summits, and scramble class 3-4 to the true summit.

- **Grade:** steep snow + class 3-4 rock; no technical pitches
- **From Lake Ouzel:** half-day round trip in good conditions
- **Snow vs rock balance:** in early-summer snow lingers high and the gully system is easier; by late August more rock is exposed and the route is more scrambly
- **Silver Glacier:** named glacier on the W aspect (per JSON ski-route notes); the standard summer climbing line uses the south aspect, not Silver Glacier proper. Silver Glacier is the ski-mountaineering line.

## Alternate route — South face from Solitude-Spickard glacier (US-only winter)
From a high camp at ~4,500 ft in upper Perry Creek basin: ascend the largest (leftmost) of four north-trending snow gullies to a low-angle shoulder at ~6,000 ft. Traverse right to a big drainage, then up to the toe of the Solitude-Spickard glacier. Traverse right on a ledge between two cliff bands to gain the south face proper, then meet the standard south-face route (low-angle slopes to almost the ridge, traverse below a steeper band, small saddle, up the ridge until blocked by gendarmes, then a snow traverse on the north face to bypass the gendarmes and reach the summit).

- **Verified by Eric Gilbertson + Nick, 2023-12-31** (south-face route in winter, 2nd Winter Ascent overall): camp-to-summit in ~6 hours on consolidated snow with thin unreactive wind slabs; ascent plates useful on the upper steeper slopes.
- This is essentially the standard south-face route reached from the south/Perry Creek side instead of the standard north-from-Ouzel side.

## Alternate route — NE ridge (winter, very rarely climbed)
Stu Johnson and Max Bond climbed the NE ridge in winter (~Dec 2022), likely the first winter ascent of Spickard. Eric Gilbertson, who looked over the ridge from the summit on his 2023 ascent, judged the NE ridge "way more difficult than what we had done" via the south face. *(Beta beyond this is unknown — see open questions.)*

## Access & logistics

The logistics differ per approach mode; below is for the standard Depot Creek approach unless noted.

- **Trailhead (standard):** End of drivable section of the logging road south off Chilliwack Lake Road in BC, just north of the US border. Road is rough and potholed; the final section to the "advanced trailhead" requires a high-clearance truck or ATV. Park where the road narrows to brush.
- **Drive from North Bend:** ~4-4.5 hrs (north on I-5, into BC at Sumas, east on Hwy 1 to Chilliwack, south on Chilliwack Lake Road to its end and beyond).
- **Border crossing:** required. The climbers' trail crosses the US-Canada border in a cleared swath; sign in at the NoCa NP self-issue kiosk on the US side.
- **Permits:** **North Cascades NP back-country permit, picked up in Sedro Woolley.** Many groups go in without one — risk is rangers patrolling and ejecting unpermitted parties despite the remote setting. Pick one up.
- **Approach distance/gain (standard):** Trailhead to Lake Ouzel ~6-8 mi, ~5,000 ft gain. Climbers' trail with one fixed-rope waterfall scramble (steep, slippery — wet-rock hazard).
- **Permits (winter / Perry Creek US-only):** NoCa NP back-country permit (Eric obtained for the 2023 winter trip).
- **Trailhead (US-only winter):** Ross Dam TH on SR 20. **Note: SR 20 closes east of mile-post ~134 in winter; verify the highway is open to Ross Dam for a winter attempt.**
- **Approach distance (US-only winter):** Ross Dam → Ross Lake (~1 mi with boat carry) → ~15 mi by boat to Little Beaver → ~3 mi trail to Perry Creek shelter → ~5 mi bushwhack + scramble up Perry Creek to 4,500 ft basin. Then summit day from there.
- **NWAC zone (per JSON):** `cascade-west-north-baker` — useful for both the standard summer south face and the winter Perry Creek approach.
- **SNOTEL (per JSON):** `909:WA:SNTL` — proxy for snowpack depth, but actual coverage at 4,500-9,000 ft on Spickard will need imagery (Sentinel-2) or recent TR confirmation in spring.

## Crux / hazards

**Access is the crux.** Once at Lake Ouzel, Spickard itself is a half-day climb at moderate grade. Everything that turns parties around is on the approach: rough logging-road driving, border-crossing logistics, fixed-rope waterfall scramble in Depot Creek, route-finding on the unmaintained trail (especially in the dark or in wet conditions).

**Depot Creek waterfall fixed rope:** wet, slippery, steep — a slip here with a heavy pack is serious. Verified hazard (Eric 2018-07-08).

**Bad weather + remote location:** the Chilliwacks sit in the wettest part of the West-North NWAC zone. The 2018 Eric/Steven trip rode out a full rain day in the tent at high camp — build flex into a 4+ day window. Whiteouts on the south face make route-finding among false summits non-trivial.

**Glacier travel:** glaciers in the area are crevassed but bridges hold well in standard summer window; rope team mandatory. In winter (Solitude-Spickard glacier), large crevasses are well-covered but the surrounding slopes can hold reactive wind slabs after storms.

**Winter US-only approach risks:** Ross Lake must be ice-free for the entire 15 mi route; satellite imagery is necessary pre-trip. Wind on Ross Lake makes inflatable-boat travel dangerous. Cold can cause boat motors to fail (verified by Eric on the 2024-01-01 paddle-out).

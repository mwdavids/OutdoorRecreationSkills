# Eric Gilbertson — Jack Mountain Nohokomeen Headwall, 2018-06-02

**Source:** [countryhighpoints.com/jack-mountain/](https://www.countryhighpoints.com/jack-mountain/)
**Author:** Eric Gilbertson + Birkan
**Date:** June 1–3, 2018 (climbed June 2)
**Route:** Nohokomeen Headwall (north side, steep snow climb — *not* in Beckey)
**Style:** car-to-car-to-car-ish: Fri pm hike to May Creek camp → Sat 18-hr summit push → Sun out

## Why this is preserved in full
- **Most directly applicable spring/early-summer beta** for a Bulger-list party from the user's Cascade base. Pure snow climb, party of 2, no guides, late-window timing.
- Documents the **bergschrund timing** that defines the seasonal window (one snow bridge with ~1 week of life left on June 2 = window closes ~June 9 in a typical year).
- Captures the **simulclimb-with-pickets-and-Micro-Traxion** technique that is the de facto modern method on the headwall.
- Eric explicitly contrasts the Nohokomeen route with the Beckey South Face route and explains why the north side is now preferred despite not being in the guidebook.

## Day-by-day
- **Fri June 1:** Drove from Seattle. Hiked from Ross Dam TH south on East Bank Trail ~8 mi (mostly flat) to a campsite near May Creek. Arrived after dark.
- **Sat June 2:** Up before dawn, **moving by ~6:30am from May Creek camp.**
  - **Bushwhack S of May Creek**: surprisingly open forest; "mercifully easy" by Cascades bushwhack standards. Climbed steep hillside, passed a small cliff band ~4,000 ft via a short scramble (game/climber trails + a tree-rap-anchor confirmed they were on the standard line).
  - **Snow at ~5,000 ft** on the shoulder. Switched to crampons.
  - **Traverse east at ~5,000 ft** to gain the Nohokomeen Glacier basin.
  - Roped up; ascended the **left side of the glacier** on gradual snow.
  - **Reached upper glacier camp area / base of headwall ~7,000 ft around 1pm.**
  - **Bergschrund:** **only one snow bridge remained** crossing the schrund at the base of the headwall. Eric estimated it would last "maybe another week" — i.e., the route would shut down for non-ice-climbing parties around June 9. After that: lower into the moat + ice climb out.
  - **Headwall:** ~50° steep snow. Simul-climbed in **3 long pitches** with pickets as intermediate anchors and a **Micro Traxion / similar progress-capture** on the second so a follower fall would not pull the leader off.
  - Some cornices on the summit ridge → **stayed right** to avoid them. Crossed/avoided rock bands on the upper face.
  - Reached ridge, dropped rope and second tools, **easy scramble to summit.**
  - Summit late afternoon.
- **Descent:** **lower-and-downclimb pattern** — placed pickets, lowered partner full pitch, downclimbed past picket, re-set, repeat. Faster than rappelling. Got off the headwall before the snow re-crusted hard.
  - Roped glacier descent.
  - Bushwhacked back down through the cliff band.
  - **Back at May Creek camp ~12:45am Sunday** = **~18 hours camp-to-camp.**
- **Sun June 3:** Slept in. Hiked the 8 mi back to the car by 11am ahead of incoming rain.

## Jack-specific takeaways
1. **The Nohokomeen Headwall is the right route for a fit Bulger party in spring** — pure snow climb, no loose rock, more aesthetic and safer than the Beckey South Face. **Not in Beckey** (so guidebook-only researchers may miss it).
2. **The bergschrund is the seasonal gatekeeper.** In a typical year the last snow bridge fails ~early-to-mid June. After that, the route requires lowering into the moat and ice climbing out — out of scope for a snow-only party. **Verify the bridge visually from down-glacier before committing.**
3. **Climbing technique:** ~3 simul pitches with **pickets + Micro Traxion** on the second. Don't pitch it out — too slow. Don't solo it — exposure is real.
4. **Descent technique:** **lower-and-downclimb is faster than rappelling** on snow of this angle. Bring enough pickets to leave one per pitch if needed (or downclimb past + re-set).
5. **Stay right on the summit ridge** to avoid cornices. Some rock bands force route choices on the upper face.
6. **The bushwhack is real but reasonable.** Open forest, small cliff band ~4,000 ft, narrow entry/exit point. Bring GPS — easy to get cliffed-out on descent in the dark.
7. **The South Face (Beckey)** is "long, involves loose 4th class rock, generally done later in summer when snow melted." North side is the better option whenever the bergschrund is passable.

## Access takeaways
- **Ross Dam TH on SR 20** → East Bank Trail south, ~8 mi mostly flat to May Creek.
- **No road shortcut into the basin** — full ~13 mi one-way to glacier base.
- Route plays well as **3-day weekend**: Fri pm hike to low camp, Sat 18 hr summit push, Sun easy hike out. Avoids carrying overnight gear up the headwall.
- **Alternative style: 2-day with glacier-camp at 7,000+ ft** on the Nohokomeen — heavier pack day 1 but shorter summit day.

## Bergschrund / window summary
| Date | Bridge status | Implication |
|------|---------------|-------------|
| Early-mid May | Likely well-bridged | Window opening |
| Late May – early June | **One bridge typical, fading** | **Sweet spot — Eric's window** |
| ~mid June | Bridge usually gone | Route effectively closed for snow-only parties |
| June – Sept | Open moat | Switch to South Face (Beckey) |

# Jack Mountain — East Ridge, July 11–13, 2025 (JDT, Damon, Jon)

## Source
- [Cascade Climbers TR by JDT, posted 2025-07-25](https://cascadeclimbers.com/forum/topic/108176-jack-mountain-east-ridge/)
- Cross-ref noted in thread: older 2008 East Ridge TR (with South Face descent) by JasonG: https://cascadeclimbers.com/forum/topic/62434-tr-jack-eastridge-9132008/ — **not yet cached**

## Why preserved
- Only the third reasonable summer route on Jack besides the South Face proper and the Nohokomeen.
- Recent (2025) beta on a "seldom-climbed" route with very little online info.
- Establishes that the East Ridge is **substantially more serious** than the South Face — multiple 5th-class pitches, sustained class 3/4 in between, "high-consequence" character. Useful as a calibration for what the user is *not* signing up for if they ever look at Jack from the east.
- Confirms the South Face still looks unappealing from above ("we also took a moment to look down at the upper part of the South Face route; it looked unappealing, reinforcing our choice of the East Ridge").

## Trip stats
- 3 days, ~25 mi total, ~12,500 ft total gain.
- Day 1: 9 mi, +5,400 / -1,400, 7h30m — Canyon Creek TH to Jerry Lakes basin camp.
- Day 2: 5.8 mi, ±4,660, **13h08m summit RT** — Jack East Ridge.
- Day 3: 9.7 mi, +2,390 / -6,420 — Crater Mountain + exit.

## Route overview

### Day 1 — Approach (Canyon Creek TH → Jerry Lakes basin)
- Same trailhead as the South Face: **Canyon Creek TH on SR 20** (~MP 141.5).
- ¼-mi in: **ford of Canyon Creek** (Crocs cached on far side recommended).
- Crater Mountain trail upward.
- **Leave trail at 6,400 ft** to ascend toward the **7,200 ft col**.
- Cross the col, descend snow below the Jerry Glacier into Jerry Lakes basin.
- Camp at the outlet of the largest lake. Scenic, clear nights, **buggy**.

### Day 2 — Jack via East Ridge
- Depart 4:30 a.m., climb a goat path out of Jerry Lakes basin to the col.
- **Descend 800 ft into the adjacent basin** before starting up the SE arm of Jack.
- Traverse over to the snowfield below the glacier.
- Few hundred feet of scrambling to **gain the East Ridge proper at 7,200 ft**.
- East Ridge: **3 distinct 5th-class pitches** + "hours" of exposed class 3/4 between them.
  - Pitch 1: gully early on the ridge.
  - Pitch 2 (crux, mid-ridge): "memorable and wildly exposed 5.0," 1,000 ft of air down to snow on the descent face-in downclimb. "Totally insane even with a few pieces of protection."
  - Pitch 3: another section to gain the summit.
- Protection described as "ok placements" (not solid).
- Summit views: Baker, Shuksan, Pickets, Ross Lake, Hozomeen, Nohokomeen Glacier sprawling below.

### Descent
- Mirror of the ascent; long, mentally taxing.
- 5.0 crux: protected downclimbing (leader places gear from below).
- **One rappel** lower down off an established tat anchor.
- Glacial basin recrossing: tried to stay on snow, **a 20-ft snow finger near the glacier toe collapsed in front of them onto rock**. Backed off, returned via scramble route. Notable objective hazard call.
- Back to camp by late afternoon — 13h day.

### Day 3 — Crater Mountain (bonus) + exit
- Crater Mtn: fun class 3 with light exposure + one unexposed class 4 move; loose rock in places (helmet useful).
- Crater summit panorama rated by author as **possibly better than Jack's**, with bonus view of Jack's South Face.
- Long hike out, river crossing "downright enjoyable in the heat."

## Crux & character
- "**A long, serious, and committing route with sustained exposure and multiple pitches of 5th-class climbing. It should not be taken lightly.**"
- "**A route for self-reliant parties with a deep well of experience in technical rock, rope systems, and managing risk in a remote, high-consequence environment.**"
- Rock quality: protection placements only "ok"; loose rock noted on Crater (similar geology, parallels the rockfall theme on the South Face).
- The **glacier-toe snow collapse** is a reminder that even the snow approach below the East Ridge has objective hazards in mid-July.

## Best window
- This party climbed **July 11–13, 2025**. Standard summer window — same broad season as the South Face but East Ridge needs the snowfield below the glacier to still be in (for the SE-arm-to-ridge transition) while wanting the rock dry.
- Mid-July likely the sweet spot. Earlier = more snow on the technical rock; later = snowfield below glacier melts out, glacier becomes more broken, and the toe collapse hazard from this TR worsens.

## Approach access notes
- Same TH and approach trail as the South Face for the first ~9 mi (diverges at the 7,200 ft col into the **adjacent basin east** rather than dropping to Jerry Lakes for the South Face attack).
- Canyon Creek ford is real (Crocs).
- 2024 South Face TR noted **Canyon Creek bridge washed out** — JDT party didn't mention this in 2025 (may have been rebuilt or they used a log/ford from the start).

| Day 2 distance | 5.7 mi | 5.8 mi |
| Day 2 gain | ~4,700 ft | ~4,660 ft |
| Day 2 time | varies, ~10 h | **13 h 08 m** |

| Dominant hazard | rockfall (shooting gallery on descent) | sustained exposure + iffy gear; glacier-toe collapse on snow approach |
| Party-size advice | 2 ideal, 3 OK, 4 too many | 2 was right (1 leader + 1 second on each pitch) |

| For the user | fallback only | **clearly out of preferred style** |

# Jack Mountain — South Face Route (Beckey) — combined references

← Peak file: [jack.md](../../jack.md)

This cache combines two complementary South Face TRs:
- **skjos** — Cascade Climbers, Aug 13 2016, 3-day. Strong tactical beta on the route through the rock face + rappel anchor.
- **WashingtonStateHiking** — Sep 28 2024, 3-day late-season ascent. Strong waypoint-by-waypoint Crater→Jerry Lakes→Jack approach beta + late-season Jerry Glacier conditions + rock-quality reality check.

**Sources:**
- https://cascadeclimbers.com/forum/topic/99842-tr-jack-mountain-south-face-8132016/
- https://www.washingtonstatehiking.com/reportslist/jack_mountain (Sep 28 2024 entry)

## Why this is preserved in full
- The South Face is the **summer / fall fallback** when the Nohokomeen Headwall bergschrund is out (after ~mid-June). The two Eric Gilbertson winter/spring TRs cached for Jack do not cover it.
- The route uses an entirely different trailhead (**Canyon Creek TH on SR 20**, ~MP 141.5) and approach (Crater Mountain trail → Jerry Lakes → south side of Jack). No overlap with the Ross Dam / East Bank / May Creek approach to Nohokomeen.
- Two independent TRs spaced 8 years apart agree on the line and the rappel anchor → the route is well-trodden enough to trust the cairns and trip reports.
- The 2024 TR documents **major Jerry Glacier melt-out** vs 2016 — the seasonal/decadal trend matters for route choice.

## Route overview
**Total ~22 mi RT, ~11,500 ft gain, 3-day standard.** Class 5 crux (~20 ft), heavy loose rock, full-fledged rockfall hazard.

### Day 1 — Canyon Creek TH → Jerry Lakes (~8 mi, ~5,300 ft gain)
- **TH:** Canyon Creek parking lot, **left side of SR 20 between MP 141 and 142** (eastbound). NW Forest Pass required. Pasayten Wilderness self-issue permit on the trail (free).
- **62 switchbacks** to the Crater Mountain fork.
- Continue past the fork, eventually getting above tree line into **Crater Lake basin**.
- **Leave the Crater Mountain trail at ~7,100 ft** (waypoint: N48.73176, W120.92059) and traverse NW to the **Jerry Glacier saddle**.
- **Cross Jerry Glacier:** mostly low-angle, was crevasse-light in 2016 (no rope used) but a "deep hole" was visible on the way back. **By 2024 the glacier had melted way out** — the 2024 party walked across rock fields where the glacier used to be. Recommend rope unless you can confirm current conditions visually.
- **Stay high** on rock/snow on the climbers' side to avoid regaining elevation, then climb to **Jerry Lake's saddle**. (skjos: "the steep side-hilling was not worth it; go a little bit lower for mellower side-hilling.")
- Descend to **Jerry Lakes** (~6,200 ft). Multiple flat campsites; west/southwestmost lake has good NE-shore camping.
- **2024 alternative camp**: tarn at 5,800 ft and tarns near 6,400 ft are fallbacks if the day blows up.

### Day 2 — Jerry Lakes → Summit RT (~5.7 mi, ~4,700 ft gain)
1. **Jerry Lakes → NW saddle**: grassy slope between the lakes, then west on a rising ledge. The 2024 party went up to ~6,400 ft then traversed SW to avoid a cliff below the saddle.
2. **Saddle → ramp down north side**: short distance west to find a ramp/ledge system descending north.
3. **Through trees**: either path through trees up high under cliffs + heather side-hill, OR drop lower and cut through at lower elevation for milder heather. (skjos preferred lower.)
4. **Up small boulder field → flat area with small pond at 5,960 ft.**
5. **Short dry drainage → very large flat area at 6,000 ft.** Stay on south banks until you climb up the south ridge.
6. **South ridge**: keep west of cliffs, gain top of ridge or stay on west slopes all the way to the **base of Jack's south rocky face**.
7. **Side-hill on snow at the base** — **bring 12-pt crampons** (skjos: "10pts are not so great for side-hilling"). Ledges above the snow eventually run out and the moat gets too deep — commit to crampons on snow.
8. **Get on the rock face**: first feasible entry is **just west of the rock protrusion at ~7,200 ft** (the one below you when looking down). In 2016 it was being "pounded with water and looked very slippery" — they used the **next entrance east**, which had a small ledge. **Important:** that next entrance is **directly under the standard rappel anchor**, so look up for the anchor as a navigation aid both up and down.
9. **Up the face**: stay east of the main drainage about halfway up, then cut across to a snow field. Crampons on snow patch. Will go a bit higher than the summit pyramid → downclimb a little to get under the base.
10. **Under the summit pyramid**: stick to the **right side / moat** of the first snow field to the top of the ridgeline.
11. **Cross briefly to back side** then **head east to summit**.
12. **2024 final-100-ft variation:** could not find a non-class-5 route directly to the summit ridge. Found a route at the **north edge of the ridge — a single low-class-5 move** onto a 30° snow slope, then easy ridge walk + small summit-block scramble. This may be the standard top-out when snow lingers.

### Day 3 — Jerry Lakes → TH (~8 mi, mostly downhill)

## Crux & hazards (consensus from both TRs)
- **Rockfall:** "On the way up the rock fall can be managed to a degree, but on the way down all bets are off, it is a full-fledged shooting gallery." — skjos. **Party of 2 is ideal, 3 OK, 4 pushing it.** If another party is already on the route, climb a different day.
- **Loose rock:** rock under the surface scree is "relatively supportive" but big flakes can pull. skjos took a large flake to the shin near the summit pyramid.
- **Class 5 crux:** ~20 ft, well-protected by a ledge below it (manageable unroped for confident scramblers).
- **Exposure:** ~triple Fernow per the 2024 TR. The 2024 partner described "far more vertical feet of exposed scrambling than any other climb I'd done." Mostly above long drops / cliffs, on rock that "rarely felt solid."
- **Above-rappel exposure:** "the most nerve-racking spot is just above the rappel — terrain is no different than anywhere else, but there is nothing below you." (skjos)
- **Top-out finish (when snow lingers):** the single low-class-5 move at the north edge of the ridge with a snow slope dropping off the **massive north face** is the most committing single move on the route. (2024)

## Rappel anchor (descent crux)
- **Rock knob, "relatively sturdy for Jack."** Backed up with wire and a couple biners; build of slings present.
- **Add a couple slings.**
- **30 m rope** → reaches the ledge below for downclimbing (the same ledge you came up on). **50 m / 60 m might reach the snow field — no promises.**
- **Anchor coords:** N48.76958, W120.95376
- **Summit coords:** N48.77285, W120.95625

## Gear
- **Helmet** (mandatory — it's a rockfall route)
- **12-point crampons** (10-pt insufficient for side-hilling on snow at base)
- **Single ice axe**
- **30 m rope minimum; 50/60 m better**
- **Anchor material** (extra slings, biners)
- **No rock pro typically needed** — the class 5 crux is short and protected by ledges; only the rappel uses gear

## Best season
- **Mid-July through mid-September** — south-facing rock route, wants snow off the moat at the base of the face but enough snow on the upper face/snow field for the standard line.
- **2016 trip = mid-August** = textbook conditions.
- **2024 trip = late September** = workable but with first snow squall on day 3 and snow lingering on the summit ridge that forced the class-5 north-edge variant.
- **Avoid** wet conditions — the slabby rock face turns dangerous when water is running on it (skjos confirmed for the lower entry).

## Approach access notes
- **Canyon Creek TH at SR 20 MP 141.5**, accessible whenever SR 20 is open (typically late May → late November).
- **2024 trip note:** **Canyon Creek bridge was washed out** at the start — they crossed on a fallen log via an old trail. Verify bridge status before trip.
- **Pasayten Wilderness permit** = free, self-issue at the trailhead board.
- **Water on approach:** "three or four stream crossings on the trail, one stream on the traverse to the Jerry glacier saddle, another stream or more on the way to Jerry lakes." (skjos) — 1 L is enough if you top off.
- **Canyon Creek is a different TH from Ross Dam** (used for Nohokomeen approach, ~MP 134). Both are on SR 20; choose by route.

## Crowding reality
- Quiet route. 2024 party saw 2 other parties at Jerry Lakes camping. 2016 party saw none on the climb.
- "Love Jack Mountain. Such a cool scramble!" — kukuzka1 in skjos comments.

## Verbatim key quotes
- **skjos on rockfall:** "On the way up the rock fall can be managed to a degree, but on the way down all bets are off, it is a full-fledged shooting gallery. A party of two is ideal, three is OK, four you are pushing your luck. If there is another party on the climb, you might want to try a different day."
- **WSH 2024 on exposure:** "this route had far more vertical feet of exposed scrambling than any other climb I'd done (about triple that of Fernow). The route weaved across the steep south slope of Jack all the way up to 8800'."
- **WSH 2024 on the rock:** "The rock rarely felt solid, which made the exposure feel much worse... most falls being lethal on the south face."
- **WSH 2024 on the final move:** "It was a single low class 5 move to get onto a 30-degree slope covered in snow. The move was super sketchy since the rock was pretty loose, and the drop off the north side of the mountain was massive."
- **skjos on the rappel anchor:** "The anchor here is a rock knob that seems relatively sturdy for Jack, and it had wire and a couple biners backing it up."
- **WSH 2024 on Jerry Glacier:** "the glacier used to be where we were walking not long ago" (≈2016-era glacier extent now bare rock by 2024).

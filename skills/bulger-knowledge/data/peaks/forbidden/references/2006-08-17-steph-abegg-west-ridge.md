# Steph Abegg — Forbidden Peak West Ridge, 2006-08-17

← Peak file: [forbidden.md](../../forbidden.md)

**Source:** [stephabegg.com/trip-reports/washington/forbidden-wr/](https://stephabegg.com/trip-reports/washington/forbidden-wr/)
**Author:** Steph Abegg + Joleen Prystupa
**Date:** August 16–17, 2006 (Wed–Thu)
**Route:** West Ridge (5.6) — late-season standard with **couloir bypass gully** variant
**Style:** overnight from Boston Basin camp (~6,200 ft)

## Why this is preserved in full
- **Second canonical source** for the late-season West Ridge (complements Eric Gilbertson 2018 cache).
- Adds **route-overlay photos** (annotated images on Steph's site) — the most-cited visual route reference for this climb. The peak file points to these.
- Concrete **timing benchmark for fast/competent parties:** 3.5 hrs camp-to-summit, 8 hrs round trip from camp. Eric's 2018 trip took ~9 hrs to summit. The spread (3.5 vs 9) reflects pure simulclimbing vs short-pitching — useful for planning.
- Documents the **couloir bypass gully** explicitly (mentioned in Beckey) — terminology question vs "Cat Scratch": Steph calls this the "couloir bypass," Eric calls his line "Cat Scratch (rightmost gully)." Possibly the same feature, possibly adjacent. **Open question to resolve.**

## Trip arc
- **Aug 16 (Wed):** Picked up camping permit at Marblemount ranger station. **6 parties/night Boston Basin quota; rarely fills midweek.** 21.7 mi drive on well-maintained Cascade River Rd to TH at 3,200 ft. Trail 3 mi / 3,000 ft to Boston Basin — steep, well-traveled, one section crosses an avalanche path. Camped at 6,200 ft. Slept out (no tent). Clouds came in at evening but dropped into the valley overnight → cloudless climb day.
- **Aug 17 (Thu):**
  - 6:40am: leave camp; first 1,400 ft is "pleasant hiking up granite slabs and flower-lined stream gullies."
  - ~1 hr in: arrive at high east side of small glacier at ~7,600 ft. **Traversed glacier high to bypass crevasses** — straightforward. (Notes it looked possible to bypass the glacier entirely by climbing further west in the basin.)
  - ~8:00am: at base of prominent 500 ft couloir leading to ridge. **Snow in main couloir too low** → took the **couloir bypass gully to the west** ("the couloir bypass route that Beckey mentions").
  - Stashed crampons / axes / boots / rain gear under a rock.
  - Simulclimbed the bypass gully: **Class 4 for first 200 ft, easing to grassy Class 3 for final 200 ft. Lower 5th class moves at the bottom. Loose** — partner pummeled by rocks; **helmets essential**.
  - **Notch reached 9:00am at 8,265 ft.** From here: 1,500 ft of West Ridge to summit, 550 vertical ft.
  - Simulclimbed the West Ridge. Solid rock, great exposure.
  - **Crux:** 5.6 tower 175 ft below summit. Clipped sling to fixed piton, belayed Joleen up to resupply rack, led tower to summit.
  - **West summit reached first, then east summit (true / higher, 8,815 ft) via airy downclimb + short traverse.** Rope got stuck → had to climb back up to fix.
  - **Total camp-to-east-summit: 3.5 hrs** (planned for 6).
- **Descent:**
  - Started 11am. Downclimbed West Ridge route using a few rappel slings encountered on the way up.
  - **Bypass technique:** "could bypass difficult moves by downclimbing on the north side of the ridge." (On the way up they kept to the exposed crest for the experience; on descent they used the N-side bypasses.)
  - From the notch, descended the same gully (rappel slings present here too).
  - Back at camp before 3pm. **Descent ~4 hrs.**
- **Total climb:** 8 hrs camp-to-camp.
- Hike out: 2 hrs of "knee-aching descent" to car.

## Forbidden-specific takeaways
1. **The "glacier" can be high-traversed at ~7,600 ft on the east side** to bypass crevasses — Steph and Joleen didn't rope up for it. Or you can avoid it entirely by climbing the basin further west. **Confirms Eric's "not really a glacier" assessment.**
2. **The standard ascent gully is the couloir bypass to the west of the main couloir** (per Beckey) when the main couloir doesn't have enough snow. This is also the descent gully (rappel slings in place).
3. **Bypass gully grade:** Class 4 for first 200 ft (with lower 5th moves at bottom), easing to grassy Class 3 for final 200 ft. **Loose — wear helmets.** Consistent with Eric's "low 5th class crux at bottom + sometimes walking in boot tracks in grass" description.
4. **Notch at 8,265 ft.** West Ridge from there is 1,500 ft of climbing / 550 ft of vertical to true summit.
5. **Crux confirmed: 5.6 tower 175 ft below the summit, fixed piton.** Same feature Eric describes as the "knife-edge → piton → 5.6 step."
6. **True summit is the EAST summit (8,815 ft)**, reached from the West summit via an airy downclimb + short traverse. **Rope can get stuck on this section** — manage carefully.
7. **All of the route can be downclimbed without rappelling** (per Steph's gear notes), though parties commonly use the in-place rappel slings to save time. Eric (2018) used ~5 rappels.
8. **Descent technique:** stay on the north side of the ridge to bypass difficulties. Climber's choice — exposed crest going up for the position, N-side bypass coming down for speed/safety.

## Access takeaways
- **Marblemount ranger station** for Boston Basin permit. **6 parties/night quota; midweek rarely fills.** (Weekends = "may as well replace 'classic' with 'crowded'.")
- **TH at 3,200 ft** on Cascade River Rd, 21.7 mi from Marblemount when road is open to MP 22. *(2026 status: gate at MP 20 per NPS NOCA alert — see peak file.)*
- **Trail: 3 mi / 3,000 ft of gain to Boston Basin.** Steep, well-traveled, one avalanche-path crossing. Camp at ~6,200-6,400 ft.

## Gear notes (Steph's recommendation for the route)
- **Rack:** 6 cams BD #.3 to #3 (used #.3, .5, 1, 2 most), set of nuts (didn't place any), 10 slings (3 longer), normal biner count. *"It is probably possible to safely protect the route using only slings, so only a minimal rack is needed."*
- **Rope:** 45m rope was just sufficient for the in-place rappel stations on West Ridge and descent gully. (Implies 50m+ is comfortable; 60m gives margin but isn't required.)
- **Helmets mandatory** for the loose ascent/descent gully.
- Crampons + axe needed for the snow approach but stashed at base of gully.

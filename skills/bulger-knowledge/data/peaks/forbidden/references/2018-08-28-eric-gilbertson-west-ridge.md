## Why this is preserved in full
- Most directly applicable Eric Gilbertson Bulger TR for Forbidden — late-season, standard route, party of 2 (not guided), with explicit pacing and rope-management beta useful to a competent self-led party.
- Documents the **late-season variant**: Cat Scratch gully (rock) instead of the early-season snow gully. This is the version most relevant for a North Bend day-tripper looking at August/September conditions.
- Captures realistic crowding picture (Boston Basin permit quota of 6 groups vs how many parties actually appear on the route).

## Day-by-day
- **Aug 27:** Drive Seattle → Boston Basin TH (squeezed in roadside parking). Left car ~1pm, hiked steep climbers' trail to camp at ~6,400 ft. **Stream crossings late-season were no problem** (sketchy in higher water). One other tent at camp (a guided party).
- **Aug 28:** Up at 5am. From camp, scramble wet slabs to base of south snowfield. Snow icy → crampons. **Snowfield is not a real glacier** (no real crevasses, no need to rope up).
  - Followed crampon tracks up steep icy snow to base of Cat Scratch gully system.
  - **Tricky moat crossing** at the bergschrund/moat at the top of the snowfield.
  - Climbed loose choss to base of steeper rock, then roped up.
  - Led up the **rightmost of the gullies**: bottom pitch was steepest (low 5th class), passed a low rap anchor, belayed at a 3-piton anchor on a ledge to the right.
  - Two more rope-lengths up the gully (sometimes walking in boot tracks carved out of grass).
  - Reached small bench, scrambled 3rd class to crest of West Ridge.
  - **On the ridge:** roped back up. Two guided groups visible. Ridge is **long** — efficiency matters.
  - **Pacing technique used:** lead out ~1/3 rope length with rest kiwi-coiled around shoulder; weave rope around horns + 1-2 pieces; belay partner up while still in sight/sound. "This is how the guides ascend the West Ridge, and it worked pretty well." Mostly 4th and low 5th class on solid rock with good holds and big belay ledges.
  - **Crux near top:** knife-edge ridge to a piton, then 5.6 step to a good ledge.
  - Reached false summit, then traversed/passed guided groups going down.
  - **True summit:** requires downclimbing 15 ft from false summit then crossing a steep ridge. Eric notes "this is one of only about five Bulgers that I have encountered another party on the summit" — i.e., genuinely crowded for the Cascades.
  - Summit at ~2:30pm.
- **Descent:** combination of short pitches and simulclimbing, plus **~5 rappels at fixed anchors** where steep enough.
  - **Rap line:** down a small arête climbers' left of the climbed gully — out of rockfall danger zone. Multiple fixed anchors.
  - At base of gully, the **boulder rap anchor at the moat had been removed** by another party — had to scrounge gear to build a replacement (warning: don't count on the lowest anchor being there).
  - By the time at the snow, it had refrozen → built nut anchor at top of cliff above snow, rapped down, then ice-axe anchor + lowered partner full rope-length to rock slabs.
  - Walked down slabs to camp after dark.
- **Aug 29:** slept in, packed out, drove back to Seattle.

## Forbidden-specific takeaways
1. **Late-season standard is Cat Scratch gully** (the rock variant), not the snow gully. Early-season the gully is filled with snow and gives a different experience.
2. **The south "snowfield" is not a real glacier.** No need for glacier travel gear / rope team for crevasse rescue — but crampons + axe mandatory when icy.
3. **Moat crossing at the top of the snowfield** is the consistent transition crux — variable per year/conditions.
4. **Ridge climbing technique:** kiwi-coil + short pitches + weaving rope around horns is the way. Full pitches waste daylight on the long ridge. Mostly 4th + low 5th, big ledges.
5. **Crux is the knife-edge → 5.6 step near top** — single piton in place.
6. **True summit requires a 15 ft downclimb + traverse from the false summit.** Many parties skip this; Bulger-list completionists must do it. Stay roped through this — Eric let other party clip his gear to save time.
7. **Descent is the time sink.** Mix of short pitches, simulclimbing, ~5 rappels at fixed anchors. Plan for it taking nearly as long as the ascent.
8. **Rap line is climbers' left of ascent gully** on a small arête — out of rockfall zone. Find this; don't rap the gully you climbed.
9. **Don't trust the lowest rap anchor exists** — Eric's was stolen. Carry enough gear to build one at the moat.
10. **Refreeze on descent:** if you summit late, the snowfield will be ice on the way down. Bring a small rack adequate for nut/axe anchors at the top of the snow.

## Access takeaways
- **Boston Basin permit quota: 6 groups/night.** Eric's party got one for a Monday (weekday helps).
- **Camp at ~6,400 ft** in Boston Basin (flat campsite area).
- **Late-summer roadside parking** at Boston Basin TH is tight — be willing to squeeze in.
- **Stream crossings late-season are easy**; high-flow can make them sketchy.
- The route is a "**50 Classics of North America**" line → expect more parties than typical North Cascades alpine routes despite the permit quota (many do it C2C without permit).

## Crowding reality
Eric saw two guided groups on the route the same day even though only one other tent was at camp. Conclusion: **C2C parties dominate the headcount on the West Ridge**, and Boston Basin permit count is not a useful indicator of route traffic.

> "In the early season the standard route is to ascend a snow gully to gain the west ridge, but in the later season when the snow has melted out the standard route is to climb Cat Scratch gully to the ridge."

I wove the rope around horns whenever possible and put in one or two pieces, then belayed Katie up while we could still see and hear each other. This is how the guides ascend the west ridge, and it worked pretty well. The terrain is mostly 4th and low 5th class climbing, with solid rock, good holds, and big belay ledges."

## Summary
Hoodoo Peak is one of the easier mountains on the Washington Bulger List but should not be underestimated—the 12-mile approach with 4,200 ft of gain demands respect. The summit ridge is a constant Class 2 boulder scramble with minimal exposure but requires careful foot and hand placement to avoid loose rocks. Notable for exceptional 360-degree panoramic views: on clear days you can spot at least 30 Bulger summits including Raven Ridge, Oval Peak, Courtney Peak, Star Peak, the Enchantment Range, and many northern Cascades peaks. Remote location in Okanogan County means minimal crowds—likely to have the summit to yourself.

**Elevation:** 8464 ft. **Prominence:** 1624 ft. **Coordinates:** 48.2518 N, -120.3426 W.

**PeakBagger link:** https://www.peakbagger.com/peak.aspx?pid=1958

## Best-Season Window
**Peak seasons: late May through early October.** Early June is especially delightful when Indian Paintbrush and Balsamroot bloom along the lower trail. Spring (May) approach features snow in the gully; late summer/fall (August-September) is drier with more stable boulder conditions. Late September and early October can be especially beautiful here when the larches turn gold around the Libby Lake approach and nearby east-side basins. Winter approach possible but significantly more committing.

## Standard Route: Libby Lake Trailhead (Southwest Ridge)

**Difficulty:** Class 2 (boulder scramble)  
**Distance:** 12 miles (RT)  
**Elevation Gain:** 4,200 ft  
**Estimated Time:** 9–11 hours (depending on fitness and boulder negotiation)

### Approach
From Wenatchee, head east on Route 2/97 for ~2 miles, then left onto WA-97 toward Okanogan for ~53 miles. Turn left onto WA-153 for 21 miles. Left on Libby Creek Road for 2.5 miles, then slight left onto Black Pine Lake Road (becomes NF-43). Continue 3.2 miles (watch for cattle on road), slight left onto NF-4340 for 1.2 miles, right onto NF-700 for 1 mile to Libby Lake Trailhead sign on left. Road is well-maintained but narrow in places.

### Summit Route
**Lower Trail (0–5+ miles):** Start at Libby Lake Trailhead and follow the well-maintained trail with switchbacks. Route is narrow initially through Indian Paintbrush and Balsamroot field (especially colorful in early June). Cross Libby Creek twice and one tributary; continue until you spot the tributary again—this is the off-trail cue.

**Off-Trail Scramble to Ridge (5–8 miles):** From the final tributary crossing, leave trail and scramble up toward the ridge between Mission Peak and the ridge leading to Libby Lake/Hoodoo. This section can be hard to navigate in spring due to snow. Ascend to the ridgeline—in spring this is a snow-filled gully; in summer/fall, mostly boulders.

**Summit Ridge (8–12 miles):** Once on the ridge, constant Class 2 boulder scramble to summit. **Critical:** check all rocks before putting weight on them—not all boulders are stable. When nearing summit, stay **right of the summit block** for easiest final ascent.

**Exposure:** Minimal exposure overall, but careful foot/hand placement is essential to avoid twisted ankles or rockfall.

### Descent
Reverse route down the ridge and gully. Same care required on loose boulders. Typical descent time slightly faster than ascent.

## Equipment
- **Hiking boots with good ankle support** (critical for boulder scrambling)
- **Trekking poles** (helpful for river crossings and rough terrain)
- **Early season (May–June):** Microspikes or crampons for snow gully; ice axe recommended if snow > 2 ft deep or if temperatures are cold
- **Summer/fall:** Standard day-hike gear
- **Layers:** Mountain weather can change rapidly
- **Sun protection:** Peak receives significant sun exposure; sunscreen essential
- **Water:** Libby Creek is reliable but carry 2–3 L capacity given 12-mile distance
- **No rope required** (Class 2 scramble)

## Alternates / Variations

**Southeast Face (Easier Alternative):** According to local beta, the Southeast Face offers a much easier route to the summit than the Southwest Ridge. The scramble is less tedious due to reduced boulder negotiation compared to the standard route. This variant may be preferable for climbers sensitive to loose rock or who prefer continuous snow (spring) over boulder work. Route-finding is less established; local knowledge or recent trip reports recommended.

**Winter Approach:** Possible but significantly more serious. Avalanche hazard in the gully section, and whiteout navigation risk. Not recommended without substantial winter mountaineering experience and current avalanche report review.

## Access Notes & Permits
**Wilderness Area:** Pasayten Wilderness (requires free NW Forest Pass + Pasayten self-issue backcountry permit).  
**Road Access:** Libby Creek Road and forest roads to trailhead are typically open year-round but may have mud/snow in early spring or after heavy rain. Forest Service occasionally gates access; check current status with Tonasket Ranger District.  
**Trail Condition:** The major blowdown on Libby Lake Trail that persisted until summer 2014 has been cleared. Trail is now in good condition. Report any new downed trees to USFS.  
**Seasonal Note:** Road to trailhead may be rough or inaccessible during heavy snow periods; best access late May through October.

## Tactical Notes
- **Start early:** 12-mile round trip requires long day; 6–7 am departure recommended for 9–11 hour outing
- **Party size:** Solo to small group (2–4) ideal; trail is well-established but remote
- **Water:** Libby Creek is reliable source (filter/treat); occasional tributaries on lower trail
- **Hazards:** 
  - Loose boulders on summit ridge—test stability before committing weight
  - Biting ladybugs reported on summit (late summer); bring insect repellent if sensitive
  - Limited shelter; afternoon thunderstorms possible in summer—have plan to descend quickly if weather deteriorates
  - Off-trail route-finding in spring snowmelt can be obscured; study geography before trip
- **Return Target:** Daylight descent mandatory given remote location; plan to be off summit by mid-afternoon
- **Solitude:** Expect minimal to no other parties given distance from population centers

## Combinations
Hoodoo's remote location and excellent panoramic views make it a logical focus for a single-peak day. The nearby peaks visible from summit (Raven Ridge, Oval Peak, Courtney Peak, Star Peak) are generally accessed from different trailheads and would require additional car shuttles. **Solo Hoodoo day trip is the standard itinerary.** Multi-day trips using Libby Lake area as a base camp could theoretically access Mission Peak (adjacent to trailhead) or explore the Pasayten Wilderness further, but these require separate route research and are not commonly combined.

# North Gardner Mountain

**Bulger #20 · 8,962.8 ft (LIDAR) · Prom 4,019.5 ft · Okanogan County Highpoint · Methow Mountains Highpoint · Gardner Range Highpoint**

## Status
Unclimbed (as of April 2026; user queried April 20, 2026).

## Personal Context
North Gardner + Gardner form the classic Methow combo from Wolf Creek TH. Strong early-season window (May–early June) with snow coverage; excellent late-season (July–October) snow-free. Most parties do both peaks in a 2–3 day trip from Gardner Meadows camp. Part of the Goldman Combo List (#4).

## Summary
North Gardner Mountain is the **Okanogan County Highpoint** and **Methow Mountains Highpoint** — a prominent 8,962 ft ridge crest in the Lake Chelan–Sawtooth Wilderness, 0.83 mi north-northwest of Gardner Mountain. The standard ascent is a straightforward Class 1–2 ridge scramble from Gardner Meadows via Wolf Creek Basin and a broad scree/snow bowl. **Most parties combo with Gardner Mountain** (see Combinations below). The summit offers panoramic views of the North Cascades, Chelan-Sawtooths, and surrounding ranges.

**Elevation:** LIDAR 8,962.8 ft (PeakBagger, revised from JSON 8,956 ft). **Prominence:** Clean prom 4,019.5 ft (line parent Jack Mountain; revised from JSON 3,996 ft). **Key col:** Nickol Creek Pass, 4,943.3 ft.

## Best-Season Window
**Late May through mid-October** (snow-free or manageable snow).
- **Snow-climbing window (May–early June):** Snow coverage continuous from trailhead to high camp. Wolf Creek Basin and scree bowl filled in; crevasse hazard minimal on North Gardner proper. Good refreezing nights. 2–3 day trip ideal.
- **Hiking season (July–October):** All snow melted. Trail dry, meadows in full bloom (July), scree solid. 3-day trip allows relaxed pacing; 2.5-day doable for strong parties. September brings cooler nights and lower bug pressure, and late September into early October can be especially beautiful when larches in the Gardner country turn.
- **Early season (April–mid May):** Possible with deep snowpack and steep slopes; bushwhacking on Wolf Creek Trail can be severe if alder not yet fully melted. Bergschrund hazard on upper snow slopes variable year to year.
- **Late season (November onward):** Avalanche risk high on steep approach slopes; snow cover inconsistent. Not recommended.

## Standard Route: Southeast Bowl–South Ridge via Wolf Creek
**Distance:** ~24–26 miles RT from trailhead. **Elevation gain:** ~7,300 ft. **Class:** 1–2 scramble with some Class 3 moves on ridge crest. **Time:** 3 days (overnight at Gardner Meadows or Abernathy Trail junction).

### Approach: Wolf Creek Trail to Gardner Meadows
- **Trailhead:** Wolf Creek TH (2,950 ft), west of Winthrop on Wolf Creek Road #5005 (well-graded gravel, suitable for regular car).
- **Trail:** Wolf Creek Trail follows Wolf Creek drainage north, with moderate ups and downs. ~10 miles to Gardner Meadows, ~2,950 ft elevation gain. Passes Wolf Creek & North Fork Trail junction, Abernathy Trail junction, Hubbard Creek Trail junction. Trail in good shape most seasons; post-2021 Wolf Creek Fire left sections sun-exposed with blowdown hazard — negotiate with caution, especially in peak summer heat. Late May (snow-free trail but good coverage above meadows) is ideal for pacing.
- **First camp:** Gardner Meadows (~6,000 ft), traditional campsites on edge of meadow in shelter of trees. Water plentiful. 6–7 hours from TH.

### Summit Day: Gardner Meadows → North Gardner
**From camp:** Hike up open meadows, then ascend grassy slopes NW toward **Wolf Creek Basin** (off-trail but obvious). Gain basin ~7,200 ft; contour to **Northeast Bowl** — a large snow-filled gully (June) or scree bowl (late summer). Boot or scramble up broad snow bowl to reach **Point 8,487 ft** (false summit on South Ridge), ~2 hrs from camp depending on conditions.

**South Ridge traverse:** From Point 8,487, true summit lies along South Ridge to NW. Mostly Class 1–2; some short Class 3 scrambling, staying on or left (west) of crest. Avoid gendarmes by trending left. One memorable ice-axe worthy slope if still snow-covered (late May–early June); late-season parties may boot-scrabble. Ridge travel mostly on scree; rock solid and stable.

**Summit:** Reached early afternoon. Small summit; views expansive (Goode, Silver Star, Baker, Shuksan visible west; Dome Peak and Mt. Logan to southwest; heart of North Cascades spread below).

**Summit RT from camp:** ~5 hours, moderate pace.

### Descent
Retrace Southeast Bowl / South Ridge. Scree surfing possible late July–August on descent from Point 8,487 (excellent), but avoid sun-exposed slope on ascent (recommend counterclockwise Gardner→North Gardner traverse for efficiency; see Combinations).

**Option:** If camping a second night before exiting, drop to Abernathy Trail junction (1.7 hrs from Gardner Meadows camp) to shorten final day's exit hike to ~7 mi / 3.6 hrs.

## Gardner Mountain (Combo Peak)
See [gardner.md](gardner.md). Both peaks typically climbed in **one summit day** (sub-6 hrs RT from Gardner Meadows high camp, May timing; 5–6 hrs late summer).

## Alternates / Variations
**Early-season (April–May) with deep snow:**
- Climb direct from Gardner Meadows to North Gardner via upper snow bowl, skip Point 8,487 false summit. Steeper scree/snow; more direct but requires boot-pack efficiency and ice-axe skill.
- 2–day trip feasible with bivy on upper snow (Point 7,800 ft reported).

**Late-season snow-free:**
- Standard route unchanged; recommend counterclockwise Gardner→North Gardner for scree efficiency.

## Access Notes & Permit
- **Trailhead:** Wolf Creek Road #5005, open year-round; road condition varies by season (best May–October). ~4 miles west of Winthrop on Twin Lakes Rd / Wolf Creek Rd.
- **Wilderness:** Lake Chelan–Sawtooth Wilderness. No permit required for day hikes; backcountry campsites require free **Okanogan-Wenatchee National Forest permit** (self-issue at TH).
- **2021 Wolf Creek Fire impact:** Trail section ~miles 3–8 sun-exposed with blowdown and burned snags. Route-finding straightforward; allow extra time in heat of summer.
- **Water:** Wolf Creek Trail and Gardner Meadows have reliable water. Upper bowl may dry late summer; carry extra.

## Combinations
**Gardner + North Gardner combo (standard):**
- Same trailhead, shared approach to Gardner Meadows
- **Optimal order:** Gardner first (south-facing slopes, warm scree descent), then traverse to North Gardner via ridge (Class 2–3, one mandatory ice-axe crossing if spring). Descent from North Gardner via upper snow bowl back to Point 8,487, then scree-surf down to Gardner Meadows. Sub-6 hr RT from high camp (May); 5–6 hrs late summer.
- **Alternative:** North Gardner first, skip Gardner Traverse (crux); drop to lower col, then ascend Gardner SE slopes directly. Longer but avoids ridge crux; suitable for parties uncomfortable on exposed scramble.

**Bonus: Jack Mountain day trip from high camp:**
- Line parent; ~6 mi RT from Gardner Meadows, ~4,000 ft gain. Nohokomeen Glacier approach (N face) or South Face (Beckey). Beyond scope of this brief; see [jack.md](jack.md).

## Tactical Notes
- **Late May is sweet spot:** Refreezing nights stable upper bowl; continuous snow coverage from TH minimizes bushwhacking; wildflowers at peak bloom on Wolf Creek Trail.
- **June turnaround recommended:** Bergschrund on upper bowl typically bridges until early–mid June; post-June melt-out can expose unconsolidated scree under wet snow on ridge.
- **Solo/party of 2:** Safest for scree surf descent; larger parties risk rope-tangle on confined ridge.
- **Weather:** High-pressure pattern (NE wind on ridge, clear nights) strongly favors refreezing / compact snow. Low-pressure = slushy mornings, avalanche hazard higher on steep snow bowl.

# Jack Mountain (9,066 ft, Bulger #14)

## Personal status
- **Climbed:** no
- **Attempts:** none
- **Style preference:** spring snow climb / ski mountaineering via the **Nohokomeen Headwall** (north side). The summer South Face is not appealing — long, loose 4th class. The Nohokomeen window is the right move for this peak.

## Summary
A massive, remote 9,066 ft Skagit Range peak above the east shore of Ross Lake — **#5 prominence in WA at 4,211 ft**. Two practical routes: the (Beckey) **South Face** (loose 3rd/4th class, snow-free, summer/fall) and the de facto modern standard for Bulger-list parties, the **Nohokomeen Headwall** (north side, steep snow climb, spring), which is *not in the Beckey guide* but has become the route of choice — pure snow climbing, no loose rock, more aesthetic. The challenges are not the climbing itself but: (a) the long all-on-foot approach from Ross Dam TH (no shortcut, no road into the basin), (b) the **bergschrund at the top of the Nohokomeen Glacier** which closes the route in summer, and (c) the commitment of a remote, multi-pitch, ~50° steep snow headwall that gets icy/exposed by midday.

## Best-season window
- **Mid-May through mid-June:** **prime window for the Nohokomeen Headwall.** Snowpack consolidated, Ross Dam approach snow-free, headwall still in snow with bergschrund passable. Eric Gilbertson's June 2 ascent (2018) caught the bergschrund with **one snow bridge "maybe lasting another week"** — that's the late-window edge.
- **Late June – mid July:** bergschrund typically opens up; route shuts down for non-ice-climbing parties. After that summer parties switch to the South Face.
- **Winter (Jan–Feb):** Nohokomeen has been climbed in winter (Anderson + Wald 1981 = first winter; Gilbertson + Stoddard Jan 25-26 2025 = second). Requires stable cold snowpack on a steep N-aspect — uncommon. Out of scope for a standard objective.
- **Mid-summer / early fall:** South Face is the standard play if going late-season — not the user's preferred style on this peak.

## Standard route — Nohokomeen Headwall (spring snow climb)
From Ross Dam TH on SR 20 (~MP 134):
1. **East Bank Trail south to May Creek (~8 mi, mostly flat)** — fast hiking trail along east shore of Ross Lake.
2. **Bushwhack up south side of May Creek** — surprisingly open forest. Climb steep hillside, pass through a small cliff band ~4,000 ft via climber/game trails (rap anchor on a tree marks standard route). Snow line typically ~5,000 ft in spring → switch to crampons/skis.
3. **Traverse east at ~5,000 ft to gain Nohokomeen Glacier basin**, ascend gradual snow slopes on the **left (west) side** of the glacier. Rope up where appropriate. **Standard glacier-camp at top of glacier ~7,800 ft** — flat tent sites, dramatic Ross Lake / Pickets sunset view.
4. **Headwall:** ~3 simul-pitched rope-lengths of ~50° steep snow up to the summit ridge. Crux = **bergschrund** at base. In good conditions: kick steps, picket-protected simul-climbing with a Micro Traxion or similar device on the second.
5. **Summit ridge:** drop rope and extra tools at the ridge, easy scramble to summit. Ridge is partly melted out, partly snow.
6. **Descent:** simul-downclimb the headwall with picket placements, or lower partner full rope-length per pitch. Ski/foot down glacier and back through the woods.

- **Grade:** AI2 / steep snow ~50° on headwall; light glacier travel below
- **Length:** ~25 mi RT + ~8,000+ ft gain → realistic as 2-day with glacier bivy, or punishing single push from May Creek camp (Eric 2018 = 18 hours camp-to-camp)
- **Gear:** rope, 3-4 pickets, ice axe + second tool, crampons, helmets, micro traxion or similar; small rock rack helpful for late-season anchors when rocks poke through

## Alternate routes

### South Face (Beckey standard) — summer/fall fallback
**Different trailhead:** **Canyon Creek TH on SR 20, MP 141.5** (NW Forest Pass; Pasayten Wilderness self-issue permit on trail). Standard 3-day, ~22 mi RT, ~11,500 ft gain.

- **Day 1:** 62 switchbacks → past Crater Mountain fork → Crater Lake basin. Leave Crater Mtn trail at ~7,100 ft, traverse NW to Jerry Glacier saddle, cross **Jerry Glacier** (significantly melted out by 2024 — much is now bare rock), climb to Jerry Lake's saddle, descend to **Jerry Lakes camp ~6,200 ft**.
- **Day 2 (summit RT, ~5.7 mi, ~4,700 ft):** NW saddle above lakes → ramp/ledge system descending north → through trees and heather → boulder field to pond at 5,960 ft → flat area at 6,000 ft → south ridge keeping west of cliffs → base of Jack's south rocky face. **Side-hill on snow at base — 12-pt crampons mandatory.** Get on rock face just east of the rock protrusion at ~7,200 ft (this entry is **directly below the standard rappel anchor** — useful nav aid). Up the face, east of main drainage, cut across to snow field, stay right/moat side under summit pyramid, cross to back side, head east to summit.
- **Day 3:** out same way.
- **Crux:** ~20 ft class 5 section (well-protected by ledge below). When snow lingers on the summit ridge, a **single low-class-5 move at the north edge of the ridge** onto a 30° snow slope above the massive north face is the most committing single move on the route.
- **Rappel:** rock-knob anchor at N48.76958, W120.95376 (summit at N48.77285, W120.95625). 30 m rope reaches a downclimbable ledge; 50/60 m might reach the snow field. Add slings to the existing wire/biner anchor.
- **Hazards:** **rockfall is the dominant hazard** — "a full-fledged shooting gallery" on descent. **Party of 2 is ideal**, 3 OK, 4 pushing it; if another party is on route, climb a different day. Exposure is roughly **3× Fernow** in vertical feet of exposed scrambling on rock that "rarely felt solid."
- **Best window:** **mid-July through mid-September.** Late September is workable but risks early snow on the summit ridge and the moat-at-base getting awkward.
- **Why it's the fallback, not the preference:** loose, exposed, rockfall-prone — exactly the style the user is avoiding on this peak. Use only if the Nohokomeen bergschrund is blown and a Bulger tick is the priority.
- **Full beta:** [South Face combined references (skjos 2016 + WSH 2024)](jack/references/south-face-skjos-2016-and-wsh-2024.md)

### East Ridge — serious alpine rock route (summer)
**Same trailhead and Day 1 as the South Face** (Canyon Creek TH, MP 141.5 → 7,200 ft col → Jerry Lakes basin camp). The East Ridge diverges from the South Face attack on Day 2 by **descending 800 ft from the Jerry Lakes col into the adjacent basin east**, then climbing the **SE arm**, traversing the snowfield below the glacier, and scrambling onto the **East Ridge proper at 7,200 ft**.

- **Stats (JDT party, July 11-13 2025):** 25 mi / 12,500 ft total over 3 days. Day 2 summit RT = 5.8 mi, ±4,660 ft, **13 h 08 m**.
- **Technical:** **3 distinct 5th-class pitches** (gully early; "wildly exposed 5.0" mid-ridge crux; gain-the-summit pitch) plus **hours of sustained class 3/4 between them** on a fiercely exposed ridge. Protection only "ok" — not solid placements.
- **Descent:** mirror of ascent. 5.0 crux is downclimbed protected from below. **One rappel** off an established tat anchor lower down.
- **Objective hazard note:** the snowfield/glacier-toe traverse is real — the JDT party watched a **20 ft snow finger near the glacier toe collapse** in front of them; they reversed and used the scramble line.
- **Best window:** mid-July (snowfield in for the SE-arm transition, but rock dry). Earlier = snowy rock; later = glacier toe more dangerous.
- **Why it's not in play for the user:** sustained 5th-class with iffy gear in a remote, high-consequence setting. **Substantially more serious than the South Face.** This is the route to know exists (so you don't accidentally end up on it) rather than the route to choose.
- **Bonus tag-on:** **Crater Mountain** on the way out is class 3 + one class 4 move with arguably the **best summit panorama in WA** — worth doing whether or not you tagged Jack.
- **Full traverse variant** (East Ridge up, South Face down): documented in the [JasonG 2008-09-13 TR](jack/references/2008-09-13-jasong-east-ridge.md). Avoids the East Ridge downclimb/rappel but the South Face descent is long, tedious, and route-finding-intensive without prior up-knowledge. **Late-season caveat:** with the Jerry Glacier broken out (mid-Sept+), gaining the ridge requires wet, loose 4th class instead of straightforward snow.
- **Full beta:** [East Ridge — JDT/Damon/Jon, 2025-07-11](jack/references/2025-07-11-jdt-damon-jon-east-ridge.md) and [East Ridge up + South Face down — JasonG, 2008-09-13](jack/references/2008-09-13-jasong-east-ridge.md)

## Access & logistics
- **Trailhead:** **Ross Dam Trailhead** on SR 20 at ~MP 134, near Diablo Lake. ~3 mi paved trail down to Ross Dam, then East Bank Trail south.
- **Drive from North Bend:** ~3.5–4 hrs (I-90 → I-405 → I-5 → SR 20 east to Ross Dam TH). **Note: SR 20 west of Ross Dam (Burlington side) is open year-round.** The seasonal closure on SR 20 is *east* of Ross Dam (Ross Dam → Mazama, closed roughly Nov → late April/May depending on snow year), so **Ross Dam TH itself is accessible from the west all year**.
- **Late-season SR 20 status (per user memory):** "SR 20 closed Silver Star → Ross Dam until ~late May." Ross Dam TH sits at the *western* edge of that closure — drive in from Burlington side any time. If approaching during the closure, no impact; if approaching from Mazama side, you must wait for SR 20 to open.
- **Winter approach variant** (per Eric 2025): if the East Bank TH access road (a short spur) is gated, **bike 4 mi down SR 20 from MP 134 closure to East Bank TH**, then hike the snow-free East Bank Trail.
- **Approach distance/gain (Ross Dam TH → glacier camp):** ~13 mi one-way, ~7,500 ft gain to 7,800 ft camp.
- **Permits:** **NPS NOCA backcountry permit required for camping** — Ross Lake NRA / NOCA wilderness. Permits via Wilderness Information Center in Marblemount or NPS reservation system. (Less competitive than Boston Basin.)
- **NWAC zone (per JSON):** `cascade-west-north-baker`
- **SNOTEL (per JSON):** `515:WA:SNTL`
- **Style options:**
  - **Recommended: 2-day overnight** with glacier bivy at 7,800 ft on the Nohokomeen. Sunset view over Ross Lake / Pickets is a feature, not a chore.
  - **3-day variant:** Fri pm hike to May Creek camp (low), Sat summit-and-return-to-low-camp (Eric 2018 style — 18 hr push), Sun hike out. Avoids the heavier glacier-camp pack.
  - **Single-push from car:** plausible only for very fast parties; ~30+ hours.

## Crux / hazards
- **Bergschrund at top of Nohokomeen Glacier** — the make-or-break feature. Closes the route in summer; in late spring may have only one snow bridge of finite life. **Verify visually from down-glacier before committing to the headwall.** If the bridge is gone, retreat — alternative is lowering into the moat and ice climbing out.
- **Steep N-aspect snow ~50°, multi-pitch** — exposed; refreeze risk on descent if the summit push runs late. Plan to be off the headwall before late afternoon sun softens it dangerously.
- **Bushwhack route-finding** in the May Creek band of cliffs (~4,000 ft) — easy to lose on descent in the dark. **Carry GPS track**; the entry/exit point through the cliffs is narrow. (Eric: "with only a few wrong turns" even with GPS at 11pm.)
- **Glacier travel:** real crevasses on the lower Nohokomeen, especially in the right side. Stay left/center on ascent; **on descent, hug climbers' right of the glacier** to avoid the worst crevasse field (per Eric 2025).
- **Long day from May Creek camp** (Eric 2018: 18 hr round trip to summit-and-back). If single-pushing from low camp, accept that descent will be in the dark.
- **Remoteness:** no quick exit. Self-rescue mindset required.

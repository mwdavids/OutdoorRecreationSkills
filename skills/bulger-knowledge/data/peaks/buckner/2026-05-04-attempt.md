# Buckner — N Face attempt, 2026-05-04

← Peak file: [buckner.md](../buckner.md)

## Plan
- **Objective:** Mount Buckner, N Face ski mountaineering
- **Style:** car-to-car day trip
- **Approach:** Eldorado TH (Cascade River Rd gate ~MP 20) → bike to Cascade Pass TH → Sahale Arm → Sahale-Boston Col → traverse onto upper Boston Glacier → N Face

## Outcome
**Turnaround at Sahale-Boston Col.** Did not gain the Boston Glacier; did not attempt the N Face.

## Conditions encountered
- **The col traverse:** the descent/traverse from Sahale-Boston Col onto the upper Boston Glacier was extremely steep snow — impassable in the conditions of the day.
- **N Face:** not observed up close, but visually appeared to be in shape from the col vantage. The face was not the limiter.
- **Approach to col:** standard Sahale Arm spring conditions, no notable issues getting to the col itself.

## Decisions & reasoning
Turned around at the col rather than commit to the side-hill traverse. The exposure and snow quality on the traverse exceeded comfortable margin for the descent — committing meant an uncertain reversal on tired legs at the end of the day. No realistic protection or bail option mid-traverse.

## Lessons
- **The crux of spring Buckner from Cascade Pass is the Sahale-Boston Col traverse, not the N Face.** Briefings and trip planning should weight this accordingly. Public TRs from summer/fall (when this section is bare rock/scree or low-angle firn) do not represent the spring problem.
- **For a future spring attempt** — two real options:
  1. **Wait for melt-out** of the col traverse (likely late June / early July, needs verification).
  2. **Approach via Sharkfin Col** from Boston Basin — adds ~3–4 mi each way, realistically a 2-day trip.
- **Pre-trip question to add to briefing checklist:** ski-cramponing/booting capability on 50°+ side-hill snow. If marginal, the standard route in spring is off the table.

## Open follow-ups

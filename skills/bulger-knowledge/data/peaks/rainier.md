# Mount Rainier

**Bulger #1 · 14411 ft · Prom 13246 ft**

## Status
Climbed (as of May 2026).

## Personal Context
Mount Rainier is the iconic Washington peak and Bulger #1. Most accessible via Paradise on the south side (Nisqually entrance). Standard 2-day climb on established snow/glacier route; no technical rock climbing on main route. Altitude acclimatization and glacier crevasse skills required. Crowded in summer.

## Summary
Mount Rainier (14,411 ft) dominates the Cascade Range. The highest volcano in the Cascades and an iconic North American alpine ascent. The Paradise/DC route is the most-traveled path on the peak—well-established snow climbing with significant altitude gain and moderate glacier travel. Not a technical climb but demands acclimatization, fitness, and crevasse awareness. Stunning views from crater rim on clear days: Olympics, Mt. Baker, Mt. Adams, and surrounding Cascades. Why it's on the list: highest/most prominent peak in Washington, classic alpine mountaineering objective.

## Best-Season Window
**Optimal:** Mid-May through early July (stable snow, established routes, manageable crevasse danger).

**Early season (April-Mid May):** Snow is consolidated and supportive; excellent visibility of crevasses; cooler and more stable; fewer crowds. Hazard: early-season avalanche potential.

**Late season (late Aug–Sept):** More exposed rock, increased rockfall hazard, more difficult snow conditions (corn/slush), climbers required to move carefully on rock near rim. Fewer crowds. Riskier.

**Nov–April:** Winter climbing only—extreme avalanche and crevasse hazard, ice climbing skills required, very few ascents. Not recommended for standard route.

## Standard Route
**Disappointment Cleaver (DC) Route — Paradise approach.** Class 1–2. Most popular route by far. Distance: ~6–8 mi RT from Paradise TH. Elevation gain: ~9,000 ft. Time: 2 days standard (Day 1: Paradise → Camp Muir ~6–8 hrs; Day 2: Camp Muir → summit → back to Muir ~8–12 hrs; summit bid possible as single push from Muir but not recommended).

### Approach
**Trailhead:** Paradise Visitor Center (Nisqually entrance, Mount Rainier National Park). Elevation ~5,400 ft. Parking available; arrive early in summer (parking fills by 9 AM). No permit required for day use; backcountry camping requires wilderness permit (available at Jackson Visitor Center or online).

**Trail to Camp Muir:** Hike Skyline Trail (paved/maintained trail to Reflection Lakes, then snow). Follow summer trail markers and cairns through meadow (Paradise Meadow), then ascend increasingly steep snow slopes. Once on snow (typically by mid-June), use established boot tracks or set your own steps. Skylight Glacier and lower Ingraham Glacier provide established travel corridor. Route-finding generally straightforward: stay left of Wawa Glacier, aim for Camp Muir shelter (visible from lower slopes). ~4,800 ft elevation gain to ~10,188 ft.

**Camp Muir:** Stone shelter with bunks (first-come, first-served; can be full). Water often available by melting snow; bring stove. Exposed location; wind common. Acclimatize overnight.

### Summit Route
**Day 2: Camp Muir → Disappointment Cleaver → Crater Rim → Summit.**

From Camp Muir, ascend snow/glacier upslope NE. The standard route varies by season:
- **Early season (May–early June):** Ingraham Direct (shorter, more direct line) is often skiable if the Ingraham Glacier is not yet heavily crevassed
- **Mid-to-late season (mid-June onward):** Disappointment Cleaver becomes the default route once Ingraham Direct breaks up with significant crevasses. The cleaver (rocky ridge) marks a more stable path on the glacier.

Route ascends Ingraham Glacier with established boot tracks on most conditions. Reach crater rim at Crater Glacier area.

**Crux:** Altitude (~14,000 ft), fatigue, and crevasse hazard. Crevasses are common on Ingraham Glacier; crevasse complexity increases mid-season as glaciers break up. Route selection depends on current conditions—check with guide services or recent trip reports for seasonal variations. Rope-team travel mandatory.

**Summit push:** Final section from rim to true summit (Columbia Crest) is gentle walk on summit plateau, typically 30–60 min from rim. Multiple "highest points" exist; true summit is Columbia Crest on SE quadrant of crater.

**Difficulty:** Class 1–2 (mostly snow climbing, not technical rock climbing on main route). Steep snow slopes in places but no exposed rock climbing required on standard route.

### Descent
Descend same route. Descent typically faster due to glissading (boot-sliding on snow). Glissading on Skylight/Ingraham glaciers can be fast but requires control; many accidents occur on descent. Descend as rope team if possible to manage pace. Crevasse hazard remains.

Estimated descent from Camp Muir to Paradise: 4–6 hrs.

## Equipment
**Essential:**
- Mountaineering boots (insulated for cold) + gaiters
- Crampons (12-point for glacier travel)
- Ice axe (50–65 cm for self-arrest practice and snow climbing)
- Harness + rope (50m minimum, standard for crevasse rescue/team travel)
- Helmet (rockfall hazard, especially late season)
- Backpack (40–50L for approach day, 20–30L for summit day from Muir)
- Insulated layers: polypropylene/fleece base, down or synthetic midlayer, windproof outer
- Gloves/mittens (mittens for extreme cold, glove liners for dexterity)
- Goggles (UV reflection off snow; bright sun can cause snow blindness)
- Sunscreen SPF 50+ (high-altitude UV exposure)

**Optional/conditionally important:**
- Trekking poles (reduce knee stress on descent, useful on approach)
- Avalanche beacon (not typical for summer climbing but early-season considerations)
- Altimeter watch (useful for acclimatization tracking)
- Thermos with hot fluid (important for warmth and morale at Camp Muir)

**Water/Nutrition:**
- Bring water container; melt snow at Camp Muir and fill for summit push
- 2–3 liters capacity at minimum
- High-calorie snacks and meals; lighter options (energy bars, gels) preferred due to weight

## Alternates / Variations

**Emmons/Winthrop Glacier Route (northeast side) — 2nd most popular.** White River entrance (different trailhead, White River Campground near Enumclaw). Less crowded than DC. Similar time (2 days) and difficulty (Class 1–2). Ascends Emmons and Winthrop glaciers to summit. Longer approach (~3–4 hrs from White River CG to Glacier Basin) but avoids Paradise crowds. Popular alternative for those wanting quieter experience. **Very popular ski descent** — White River Road opens to campground late May or early June; ski mountaineering window May/June.

**Ingraham Direct (early-season DC variant):** Early season (May–early June) variant of the DC route, when the Ingraham Glacier has not yet broken up significantly. More direct line avoiding the Disappointment Cleaver detour. Popular for spring ascents and ski descents when snow conditions allow. Becomes impractical once extensive crevassing develops mid-season (switches to standard DC route).

**Fuhrer Finger:** Steep ice/snow couloir on south side. Grade 2–3 ice climbing. Technical alpine climb, not casual route. Requires ice climbing skills, single rope team protection typical. **Very popular ski descent in May** — excellent spring ski mountaineering objective.

**Kautz Glacier:** South/southwest side. Mixed glacier/rock climbing. Grade 2–3. Serious rockfall hazard, especially summer. Not recommended for standard parties.

**Tahoma Glacier:** West/northwest approach. **3-day expedition** with long approach and need to carry gear over to summit. Long, remote route. Significant glacier travel and crevasse hazard. Grade 2–3. Technical alpine climb. Access from Mowich Lake trailhead.

**Liberty Ridge:** North side climb. **3-day expedition** with long approach and need to carry gear over to summit. Serious alpine rock/ice route. Grade 4 mixed climbing. Exposed, significant rockfall hazard, complex routefinding. For experienced alpinists only. Access from White River area.

**Ptarmigan Ridge:** Northeast alternative. **3-day expedition** with long approach and need to carry gear over to summit. Similar difficulty to DC but significantly longer approach and more remote. Popular ski descent in spring for those with time and logistics. Access from White River area.

**Gibralter Ledges:** Direct route above camp Muir. Technical alpine climb and a favorite in winter when loose rock is covered or frozen solid.

**Winter / ski mountaineering:** Rainier is a premier spring ski mountaineering destination. Peak season: May–early June. **Fuhrer Finger:** very popular ski descent (May). **Emmons:** popular ski descent (May–June, after White River Road opens). **DC route:** skiable but less popular for descents. Ski descent from Camp Muir fun but requires avalanche awareness, ski mountaineering skills, and avalanche beacon.

**Single-push from Paradise:** Possible for fit parties who handle altitude well (16–18 hr sufferfest, high altitude without acclimatization). Increases risk significantly.

## Access Notes & Permits
**National Park:** Mount Rainier National Park. $30 vehicle pass or $15 individual. Nisqually entrance open year-round.

**Wilderness permit:** Required for backcountry camping. Available at Jackson Visitor Center (Longmire) or online at recreation.gov. Can be obtained same-day in summer but popular sites fill early. Reserve in advance for peak season (June–Aug).

**Day use:** No permit required for day climbers (climb and descend same day). Parking is the constraint—arrive very early in summer.

**Road conditions:** Nisqually Road (SR 706 to Paradise) open year-round but may have seasonal closures or weather delays in winter. In winter, the gate is often not opened beyond Longmire if there has been a lot of new snow. Check NPS website before trip.

**Current restrictions:** Check NPS website for current closure information, trail conditions, and seasonal updates. In 2026, Paradise area typically fully open by late May.

**Climbing regulations:** Maximum party size 12. Crevasse rescue skills strongly recommended or required (NPS requests but doesn't enforce). Self-rescue training highly valuable.

**Dog-friendly:** No (National Park regulations prohibit dogs on trails except service animals).

**Nearby town:** Ashford, WA (west of park, ~30 min drive from Nisqually entrance) has gas, lodging, supplies.

## Tactical Notes
**Start early:** Begin hike to Camp Muir by 6–7 AM to ensure good weather and safe descent timing. Early start (2–3 AM) from Camp Muir for summit push, back by early afternoon to avoid afternoon thunderstorms (common in summer).

**Party size:** 2–4 ideal. Larger parties move slowly and complicate crevasse rescue. Maximum recommended 4–6 for standard route.

**Altitude acclimatization:** 14,411 ft requires acclimatization. Camp Muir overnight is minimum; some climbers consider doing two nights for better acclimatization. High Elevation Cerebral Edema (HACE) and High Elevation Pulmonary Edema (HAPE) are real risks. Know symptoms.

**Crevasse hazard:** Ingraham Glacier has many crevasses. Rope team of 2–4 mandatory. Glacier travel skills (crevasse rescue, self-arrest) essential. Practice self-arrest beforehand.

**Rockfall:** Late-season rockfall can be significant on upper peak. Helmet mandatory. Move quickly on rock sections.

**Water sources:** Melt snow at Camp Muir for hydration (bring fuel); little water on descent. Stay hydrated on approach day.

**Camp Muir logistics:** First-come, first-served bunk space; can be full in summer. Plan for sleeping bag and insulation (shelter is windy, exposed). No stove/fuel provided—bring your own to melt snow for water.

**Weather windows:** Afternoon thunderstorms common in summer. Turn around if weather deteriorates. Clear morning skies typical in early season; later in season, afternoon clouds more likely.

**Return time:** Aim to be back at Paradise parking lot by 4–5 PM to allow for parking lot traffic (summer bottleneck).

**Descent control:** Glissading (boot-sliding) is fast and fun but dangerous. Control your speed. Many accidents happen on descent due to overconfidence. Stay focused.

## Combinations
Rainier is not typically combined with other Bulger peaks on the same day or trip without returning to the car. The 2-day (or multi-day remote route) commitment and logistics of the climb make same-trip alpine combinations impractical. Focus on Rainier as a standalone objective.

# Kimtah Peak (8,600 ft, Bulger #44)

## Personal status
- **Climbed:** no
- **Style preference:** TBD. Third peak from the east on Ragged Ridge. Best paired with Cosho on a single day from camp (the Kimtah→Cosho traverse is the most aesthetic section of the ridge), then Mesahchie + Katsuk on a separate day. See [mesahchie.md](./mesahchie.md), [katsuk.md](./katsuk.md), [cosho.md](./cosho.md) for the full traverse.

## Summary
Third Bulger peak going west along Ragged Ridge. PeakBagger lidar: **8,649 ft** (8,600 ft in JSON). Prominence per LIDAR: **1,119 ft** (key col = Kimtah-Katsuk col 7,530 ft). Line parent: Katsuk Peak (LIDAR clean prom 1,119 ft). Coords 48.5793 N, 120.9075 W. PeakBagger PID 1820. FA: Steve Roper & Don Swanson, **June 6, 1970**.

The Kimtah ascent gully is described by Lemke as **"nature's staircase" on solid granite that fractures into perfect cubes** — the best rock of the south-side traverse. The peak sits between Katsuk and the diminutive Cosho, with the Grotesque Gendarmes guarding the saddle to Katsuk on the east. **Region 6: Ragged Ridge.** North Cascades NP, Stephen Mather Wilderness, Skagit County.

## Best-season window
- **Mid-July through mid-September** — prime. Standard route is the south face gully — solid granite stays dry.
- **June – mid-July:** snow in the south-face gully and on the Kimtah-Cosho traverse; the "glacier" north of Thieves Peak (between Kimtah and Cosho) is more credibly crevassed.
- **Late spring (ski):** Kimtah Glacier (north side) is one of the three glaciers on the spring ski traverse. Summit not a ski objective.

## Access — Easy Pass TH (SR 20)
Same as [mesahchie.md](./mesahchie.md). Easy Pass trail 3.6 mi / 2,800 ft to 6,500 ft pass; SR 20 closure Nov–late May.

## Standard route — South Face gully (Class 2+ / 3)
Beckey CAG Vol 3 lists this as Class 2+; Lemke 2014 describes the actual standard gully as solid Class 3 granite. The route description below is from Lemke and is the modern consensus.

### Approach
1. Easy Pass TH → Easy Pass → 6,600-ft basin camp south of Mesahchie (5 h, see [mesahchie.md](./mesahchie.md)).
2. From camp, ascend 800 ft to the **7,400-ft bench** (Class 4 on the final 250 ft via ribs/gullies).
3. **Begin the south-side traverse west at the 7,400-ft level** — stays "quite friendly compared to other North Cascades traverses" (Lemke) and never blocked across the south slopes of Katsuk and Kimtah.
4. **Important: stay at ~7,400 ft** — lower elevations cannot cross some of the gullies.

### The Grotesque Gendarmes bypass (crux of the traverse, NOT the summit)
- **After ~2.5 h of traverse from camp**, arrive at a **prominent buttress and huge cliff** right after crossing a very wide talus-filled gully. The Grotesque Gendarmes are visible above near the Katsuk-Kimtah saddle.
- Bypass per Lemke (referencing earlier Tom S. CC.com TR): **descend ~150 ft down the buttress, locate a narrow gully, descend another 50 ft, round a corner.** A ledge then leads to talus below the cliff — but **the first ledge has a very airy step midway across** that Lemke's party found uneasy.
- **Continue another 50 ft down the gully to a second ledge that's easier.** Total descent for the bypass: **250–300 ft**.
- **This bypass is the crux of the whole traverse — Class 4 with exposure no matter which line.**

### Kimtah summit gully
- Past the Gendarmes, continue an ascending traverse back to the 7,400-ft contour, crossing a few more gullies.
- **Use the third major gully past the Gendarmes** — obvious, with running water (first gully past the Gendarmes that had flowing water in 2014).
- **Class 3 scrambling on solid granite that fractures into perfect cubes** — "nature's staircase." Best rock of the south side of Ragged Ridge.
- Top out on the ridge **just west of the summit** (between the highpoint and a sub-peak to the west). Continue east a few minutes with **Class 3 scrambling** to reach the true summit.
- **Mountaineers summit register** (placed fairly recently as of Lemke 2014).

### Descent
On the return from Cosho, you can **skip re-climbing Kimtah** by traversing lower on the south side from the Kimtah-Cosho col, **following cairns on a ledge system** that drops you right back at the standard ascent gully. Reverse the route.

### Stats (Kimtah + Cosho from camp)
- **Camp → Kimtah summit:** ~5.5 h on the move (Lemke).
- **Kimtah → Cosho RT + return to camp:** ~6 h additional. Full day ~11–12 h with photo stops.
- **From Easy Pass TH (c2c, Kimtah only):** ~18 mi RT, ~7,000 ft gain. Long day.

## Equipment
- **Helmet** — loose rock above and below, especially in the Gendarmes bypass.
- **30-m rope optional** — most parties solo. A handline is reassuring on the Class 4 bypass step.
- Approach shoes + ice axe (snow in some gullies into August).
- **Light alpine rack** (small cams, slings) optional for the Gendarmes bypass.

## Hazards
- **Grotesque Gendarmes bypass is the headline hazard** — 250–300 ft of Class 4 downclimbing with exposure. Reverse on the return.
- **Route-finding** on the south-side traverse — stay at 7,400 ft; deviation downward gets cliffed out.
- **Heat exposure** on the south-facing traverse in midsummer — water in the gullies is unreliable; carry full water for the day from camp.

## Ski variant
Kimtah Glacier (north side) is one of the three glaciers on the spring ski traverse (see [mesahchie.md](./mesahchie.md) Ski variant). The summit is not a ski objective. Galen Weld 2025-05 traversed Kimtah Glacier and noted **a beautiful afternoon icefall**; the saddle below Cosho (south side of Cosho summit) is the standard camp.

# Trip-Type Gear Intelligence

Per-trip-type recommendations and rules-of-thumb. Pull only the section that matches the trip. Layer the [season-overlays.md](season-overlays.md) on top.

## Alpine climbing

- Helmet non-negotiable on any route with rockfall exposure.
- Layers: wicking base → fleece/grid mid → puffy (summit) → hardshell. No cotton.
- Approach shoes vs. mountaineering boots — depends on sustained snow/ice.
- Double boots above ~12k ft cold conditions or winter alpine.
- Bivy gear (emergency sack minimum) even for day objectives — PNW weather turns fast.

## Glacier travel & crevasse rescue

Every rope-team member carries their **own** kit. Non-negotiable, even on "easy" glaciers.

Per-person minimum:
- **Rope** (team carries one): 30m 6mm static (e.g., Petzl RAD Line) for skilled teams of 3–4; 40–50m 7–8mm half/twin dynamic for two-person or less-experienced.
- **Harness** — lightweight mountaineering with opening leg loops (e.g., Petzl Tour) — fits over crampons/skis.
- **6 locking carabiners** — 1 Clepsydra-style auto-lock (clipping to rope); 1 oval for devices; 1 large HMS for Munter; 3 D-lockers. The extra locker matters mid-rescue.
- **1 small non-locking carabiner** — racking a screw or Z-drag tractor.
- **Progress-capture pulley** (Petzl Micro/Nano Traxion) — modern standard; ascender + pulley.
- **Ascender** (Petzl Tibloc) — lightweight rope grab.
- **1 ice screw** ≥16 cm (e.g., Petzl Laser Speed Light 17 cm) — clip off the rope from inside; bombproof anchor on top.
- **~3 m of 5–6 mm high-strength cord** (e.g., Sterling V-TX 5.4mm) — equalization, ascending, self-rescue.
- **1 short friction hitch** (spliced 5–6 mm or Sterling Hollowblock) — ascend + Z-drag tractor.
- **1 120 cm sling** (two-person teams) — anchor extension, equalization, improvised connections.
- **Snow picket** (OPTIONAL, e.g., SMC Pro Picket) — faster snow anchors; keeps axe free. Permanent cable on the center hole = full-strength vertical or horizontal placements. Without one, deadman the axe/skis/pack.

**Don't need:** separate belay device (Munter on HMS works), dedicated waist/leg prusik loops (cord + hitch above covers it), extra pulleys (borrow partner's Traxion for a 6:1 second sheave).

**Racking:** rope-ascending gear on the harness, not in the pack. Friction hitch, Tibloc, oval locker, Traxion + ice screw on harness. The rest in the pack lid.

## Ski mountaineering / ski touring

- **Avalanche kit:** beacon (worn, not in pack) + shovel + probe ≥300 cm. Every member. Non-negotiable.
- Skins + skin wax (spring: pollen + wet snow glop instantly).
- **Ski crampons** for firm traverses.
- Boot crampons + ice axe for bootpacking above the ski line.
- **Repair kit:** binding-specific screws, zip ties, hose clamps, Voilé strap, multi-tool with pliers. Carry a dedicated backcountry ski repair kit (e.g., Traverse Equipment) or build your own. A binding failure 8 mi in ends the day or starts an emergency.
- **Rescue sled/bivy** (e.g., Alpine Threadworks) — ~12 oz, doubles as emergency bivy; evacuates an injured partner on snow. Worth it beyond mellow terrain.
- **Snow saw** — snow study, wind shelter blocks, some cut wood in an emergency.
- **Transition kit:** dry/warm gloves, thermos for hot tea, base scraper for pollen.
- Goggles AND sunglasses (goggles for storm/wind/descent; glasses for skinning up).
- Whippet or ice axe — exposure dependent.
- **Layering for high output + cold stops:** merino base, breathable softshell or anorak for wind (full hardshell only if precip expected), puffy in the pack for stops + emergency. Bibs over pants for descent snow protection.
- **Helmet** for exposure, couloirs, trees. Should fit inside or strap to the pack on skin track.
- Sunscreen + lip balm — spring sun on snow is brutal; reapply at transitions.
- **Satellite communicator** (InReach/SPOT) — standard carry for any BC ski day.
- Cordelette + double-length sling + couple of carabiners — improvised rescue, rappelling rollover terrain, partner assist.

## Backcountry skiing (non-mountaineering)

- Same avy gear stack as ski mountaineering.
- Lighter on the technical mountaineering kit (ice axe / crampons only if exposure demands).
- Repair kit, dry-glove transition, sat comm still mandatory.

## Backpacking

- 1 tent per 2 people standard. Solo = bivy or ultralight 1P.
- Sleep: bag + pad + liner. **Pad R-value matters more than bag rating for cold ground.**
- Camp kitchen scales: 1 stove per 2–3 people, shared fuel.
- Bear canister or hang kit depending on area regs.
- Camp shoes — luxury worth the weight on multi-day.

## Day hiking / scrambling

- 10 Essentials always, even on "easy" Cascades day hikes.
- Microspikes or light crampons shoulder season (Oct–Jun for most alpine trails).
- Trekking poles — especially steep terrain + river crossings.
- More water than you think (~0.5 L per hour of activity).

## Snow camping

- 4-season tent OR bomber 3-season + snow stakes + deadman anchors.
- −10 °F to −20 °F bag + insulated pad (R-value 5+).
- Vapor barrier liner in extreme cold (below 0 °F).
- Insulated bottles or Nalgenes wide-mouth (won't freeze shut).
- **White gas stove** — canister stoves fail below 20 °F unless you warm canisters.

## Rafting / paddling

- **PFD** — non-negotiable.
- Dry bags: clothes, electronics, food.
- Wetsuit or drysuit depending on water temp (anything <60 °F = cold-water immersion risk).
- Throw bag for rescue.
- Helmet for Class III+ whitewater.
- River shoes with toe protection (not flip-flops).
- **Everything in or clipped to the boat.** If it's not attached, assume it swims.

## Trail running

- Strip to absolute essentials — every ounce.
- Hydration vest, not a pack.
- Calories: gels/chews <3 h, real food longer.
- Headlamp if any chance of being out past dark.
- Whistle + emergency blanket weigh nothing — carry them.
- Phone with offline maps + emergency contact plan.

## Quantity & weight guidance (multi-day)

| Item | Calc |
|---|---|
| Food | ~1.5–2 lbs/day backpacking; ~2.5 lbs/day mountaineering |
| Fuel | ~3 oz canister/person/day boil-only; ~5 oz/day cooking meals |
| Water capacity | Min 2 L carry; refill every 2–3 h on trail |
| Socks | 1 pair per 2 days + 1 dry reserved for camp |
| Underwear | Same as socks |
| Base layers | 1 worn + 1 dry (sleep in the dry one) |

# Trip style playbook

Trip style is not a label — it tells you which inputs matter, which to
ignore, and how to read the data you do pull. Use the matching subsection
below to shape the briefing; ignore the rest.

## Peak bag / scramble

- Weather window and visibility are the call. Ridgeline wind matters more
  than snowpack.
- Focus on precip timing, cloud cover, summit temp (lapse-rate adjusted),
  and lightning risk.
- In shoulder season: confirm via SNOTEL + recent reports whether the
  route is snow-free or still needs ice axe / crampons.
- NWAC and corn analysis usually don't matter once the snow is gone.

## Ski mountaineering (corn / spring volcano)

- The corn cycle is the centerpiece — see [corn-cycle.md](corn-cycle.md).
- Avalanche focus shifts to wet slab / wet loose; NWAC danger and problem
  type are top-line.
- Departure timing driven by **corn timing, not sunrise**. Plan the line
  to follow the sun: east early, south mid-morning, west afternoon.
- "When in doubt, be early" — wait at the top, never get caught late.
- Suggest the CalTopo sun-exposure layer for aspect timing.
- Approach tactics: steep trails minimize patchy-snow hassle; north-facing
  basins make the best exits (skiable longer, less mush late in day).
- Creek-hole hazard on snow-covered drainages later in the day — call it
  out when the approach crosses any meaningful streambed.

## Backcountry skiing (powder)

- See [powder-shelf-life.md](powder-shelf-life.md) for the temperature
  decay rules.
- NWAC danger rating is the first thing to check. High/Extreme is a hard
  no for this style.
- Wind loading patterns drive where the good snow is — pull the NWAC
  ridgeline wind data and the snow level for the zone.

## Alpine climb (technical)

- Route conditions trump general weather. Recent trip reports are the
  most valuable single input.
- Freezing level determines whether ice features are in.
- Approach conditions (snow bridges, creek crossings, brushed-in trails)
  can be the actual crux — call it out in **Seasonal Crux** when so.
- Lightning risk matters for exposed ridges; check NWS thunderstorm
  probability in any unstable airmass.

## Glacier travel

- Crevasse conditions are seasonal. Late season = open crevasses + weak
  snow bridges.
- SNOTEL SWE + % of median is a proxy for bridge strength over the
  glacier.
- Departure timing: early morning when bridges are frozen.
- Rope teams mandatory; flag if party size doesn't support self-rescue.

## Rock climbing (crag or alpine rock)

- Days since last rain, daytime highs, lightning risk = the whole story.
- Skip SNOTEL, avalanche, corn entirely.
- For shaded north-face routes: note overnight low (dew point on rock).
- For south-facing routes in summer: note when shade arrives.

## Whitewater rafting / kayaking

- USGS gauge flow (cfs), recent precip, snowmelt drivers (SNOTEL +
  recent warm spells for snow-fed rivers in spring).
- Air temp + water temp for swim/rescue planning.
- Skip avalanche, corn, ridgeline wind.
- Sun exposure on the river matters for cold-water trips.

## Trail running / hiking (snow-free)

- Precip, temps, smoke (AQI in late summer), trail conditions from
  recent reports.
- Skip avalanche and SNOTEL unless lingering snow is plausible on a high
  route.

## Seasonal context

Time of year fundamentally changes what matters.

### Winter (Dec–Feb)
Short days, cold temps, full avalanche season. NWAC is the primary
source. Most Bulgers are full-on mountaineering objectives.

### Spring (Mar–May)
Corn season on volcanoes. Freeze-thaw is king. Snow consolidates but
avalanche problems shift to wet slab / wet loose. Access roads still
closed (SR 20, Chinook).

**Consolidation progression:** south/east Cascades (Teanaway, Stuart,
eastside) consolidate first — plan corn trips there early spring, then
move north and west as season progresses. NW Cascades and high volcanoes
consolidate last but melt out last too. South/west aspects consolidate
faster than north.

**Spring road access is a gamble:** unplowed forest roads add miles of
patchy walking. Scout the evening before if uncertain — firm morning
snow on roads can become impassable mush by afternoon.

**Gear:** skin wax (or hot-wax skins the night before) is essential for
dry/wet transitions; carry a scraper for pollen on bases.

### Summer (Jun–Aug)
Peak bagging season. Snow off most routes. Weather windows frequent.
Wildfire smoke is a real factor late summer. Creek crossings tricky
during peak snowmelt.

### Fall (Sep–Oct)
Late-season scrambling window. Early storms can blanket peaks. Short
weather windows between systems. Larch season in the Enchantments
(permits!).

## Lapse rate

Standard atmospheric lapse rate: **~3.5 °F per 1,000 ft**. The NOAA grid
point is often thousands of feet below the summit. Always do the math
and show it.

## Wind at elevation

NOAA forecasts wind at the grid point, which is sheltered. Ridgeline
winds are typically 2-3× the forecast. Above 35-40 mph at ridgeline
makes alpine travel unpleasant and potentially dangerous; >50 mph is
a no-go for most styles.

## Forecast confidence

See [forecast-coverage-bands.md](forecast-coverage-bands.md) for the
calibration table. Within 48 h NWS hourly is reliable for valleys, less
so for alpine. Beyond 3 days, suggest re-checking closer to the date.
